Yes- I discovered this the other day. The key for X is to keep
moving in this case: if he keeps moving next to O, he can force a
draw. If O builds a wall between them, then O loses the wall
advantage. (X should move in this case.)

In a couple of email conversations, it's been noted that larger boards
make this game _worse_: if X can't hinder O`s movement with his piece,
X is at a serious disadvantage. I thought about adding a rule that
the player who moves second always gets one last wall placement, so
each side ends up with the same number of turns. This seems to always
lead to a draw barring horrible play.
