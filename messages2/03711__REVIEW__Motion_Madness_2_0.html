[REVIEW] Motion Madness 2.0

From: caravel@halcyon.com (Erik Hermansen)
Date: 17 09 1997 00:00:00 GMT



I liked Matthew Daly's review and thought I'd write one also for a
game that I like. Nobody put me up to writing this.

Game: Motion Madness 2.0
Publisher: Roland Curit
System Requirements: Windows 3.1/95
Web: http://www.pilot.infi.net/~rolandc

This game is entirely abstract. No story. No characters. The
graphics do little more than identify the different game elements.
Motion Madness 2.0 can really only be appreciated for the unique and
fun type of puzzle it presents.

In this game you position arrows on a grid to get these bouncing balls
to do things. The concept is something like Lemmings in that you
don't control any one object, but instead try to create routes for the
moving objects in the game.

There are puzzles that really can only be enjoyed on a computer, and
this is the case for those found in MM2. Playing MM2 is like stepping
inside a time machine that can be used to survey your problem at any
point. You can only change the initial state of the problem, by
placing arrows on the grid that will direct the balls to hit different
objects. After you have arranged the arrows in the initial state, you
can watch the action unfold with balls moving around the grid,
bouncing off of walls, running into bombs, etc.. You can move forward
and backwards one step at a time to see if your plan worked or not (it
usually doesn't). You really can't tell just by looking at the
initial state exactly what must be done to solve a problem.

There are no timers, limited lives, or arcade-style tension devices in
the game. I think other puzzle games (Lemmings, Chip's Challenge)
have been lessened by requiring quick reflexes from the player, and
was glad that, in MM2, I could construct my solutions in peaceful
contemplation.

One warning for people that download the demo: figuring out how to
play the game is a little tricky due to the interface. When I started
up the game, I was frustrated because I couldn't seem to get to the
part where I was actually playing the game. I clicked on an icon with
a brain on it and got to another screen with the game grid, but it
didn't seem to do anything. I dragged some arrows on to the grid and
then wondered what to do next. I had to read the online docs and fool
around some more before I could begin playing.

The interface could be made much clearer by just associating some text
with all the mysterious icons so that the player can figure out what
these are supposed to do.

Another minor gripe--the window and its contained elements doesn't
size properly to resolutions larger than 640x480. On my system, I
can't switch to 640x480 without restarting Windows, so I want the game
to look proper in the 1024x768 resolution that I use for my other
apps.

The other thing is that I wish there were some easier puzzles included
in the registered version. I have an appetite for puzzles that are
less difficult than the very hardest I can manage to solve. Maybe
this keeps me from joining the hardcore elite of puzzlesolvers, but I
do enjoy easier challenges. If I had my way, MM2 would have all of
the hard puzzles in it that it does now, but also a bunch of easier
ones like those in the shareware demo. You can make your own puzzles
with registered MM2, so maybe Roland or someone else will make some
easier puzzles for me and post them here.

All in all, this is a game well worth the ten bucks I paid for it..
Also, I would like it if there were other games made that used a nifty
simulation approach like MM2s.
