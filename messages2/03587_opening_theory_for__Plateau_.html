I've been thinking about opening theory for plateau. Here's the general
drift of my thinking.

There are a small number of "apparent openings"; basicly your choice of
top color
and corner or side position, for a total of 8. Likewise, there are a reasonably
small number of "apparent responses"; choice of color and either 6 or 11
possible
positions. This gives a total of 68 opening scenarios; a fairly managagable
number.

Behind each of these apparent openings, there is the real opening, including
the concealed information. For each of the 68 scenarios, there are a
similarly managible number of actual positions; about 150. If one were to
analyze all 150 (for any one scenario), some would confer an obvious advantage
to one side or the other.

The proper choice of openings involved two considerations; Choosing a scenario
that maximizes your advantage, and mixing strategies enough to avoid being
predictable,
which would confer a severe disadvantage.

One could write a program to evaluate all the openings (plus a few moves
of followup)
to give a rough expectation, and digest the data into a table of
recommendations.

The techniques for developing a properly mixed strategy are fairly well
known; the problem is essentially the same as the classic "prisoner's delema"
game, but with a much larger table of strategies and payoffs.
