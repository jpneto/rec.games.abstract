In my high school there was a weird kid with bug eyes studying to be a Chess
grandmaster. One day I sat down across from him and played. I used a total
blitzkrieg strategy, capturing at every possibility. Would you believe I
won? If you (a general you) did, you'd be a fucking idiot. Of course he
won, but I think I had him off balance momentarily.

If you (Christian) had a bunch of grandmaster hopefuls studying your game,
that base-8 base-9 business would be going through the roof. More than
that, even if it works in a practical sense, it's an aesthetic problem.

Some people *loathe* draws. I've met them. I'm not alone. I can't release
a game in which my playership even *might* have to endure a cyclic draw -
even once!

Sorry about the rambling nature of this, but - that being said - I'm not
repulsed by infinite games. Conjecture: This is hard to express so I'll try
by way of example. Let's say you take a finite subset of the infinite game
Chess - all games less than 200 moves that conclude in checkmate. This game
would be richer, i.e. its game tree would have a larger "practical use" area
than a "comparable", naturally finite game (not requiring superko), whatever
that game might be. Just rule of thumb kind of shit. I'm sure there will
be examples that contradict this.
