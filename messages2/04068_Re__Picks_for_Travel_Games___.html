Scrabble also comes out in a pocket edition. The tiles are little
squares of flexible plastic, which stick onto smooth surfaces. You
even get a little extra plastic, for cutting out replacement tiles,
should you lose any.

Someone else mentioned Checkers. You could get a set of pieces in a
"Dollar" store which all stick together, so they could fit in some
small cylindrical space. A folding chessboard & pieces probably
wouldn't take up much space. There are a LOT of chess variants, many
of which are very different from chess. Also, there are flat magnetic
sets which would fit into the fullest pack.

Connect Four is available in a travel version, about 5x4x1 inches.
There's also travel Othello, about 6x3x1 inches.

This is all buildup for the coup de grace: if you have an old Twixt
set and wouldn't mind reducing its resale value for the sake of cubic
inches, the four panels that make up the board can be made to fit
together in about half the space they normally take up. If the board
is hinged (3M), you have to destroy the hinges. Old AH sets use
separate panels which are held together by clips. You would need some
kind of clips for holding the panels together. For travel, cut two
notches on the bottom of each panel. The idea is, two panels will fit
together, bottom to bottom, slightly offside, so they will fit
together taking up a little more than half the thickness they
otherwise would have. The pieces go in baggies. God bless zip-lock.
Maybe some pieces could go inside the space between two panels. You
really need only 30 pegs & links of each color, a total of 120 pieces.
Two big rubber bands hold it all together. See how easy? :-)
