Well, I certainly can't address your feelings, but being allowed to make
multiple moves on your turn is a standard variant of many (most?)
abstract games. This can be considered a multiple-one-step-moves
variant.

> change, anyway. Assuming you allow diagonal motion, I guess.

Good point. Since we always played with lines of pencil and paper and
never with pieces on a board, we did not consider the "spots" of the
grid to be more important than considering the line itself as a
continuous barrier, so this was not allowed.

> CGT analysis? Could you give a pointer to more information on
> whatever that is, please? (Scary... I took combinatorics last
> semester and I've already forgotten how to analyze big Nim-like
> games...)

Combinatorial Game Theory, meaning the theory of games with sequential
moves, as opposed to Economic Game Theory, the theory of games with
simultaneous moves. The standard reference is _Winning Ways for Your
Mathematical Plays_, which is being reprinted in a frighteningly
expensive four-volume set of which two are now available.
