You're misunderstanding an important part of the game: that the result
of a given random event may depend on the non-random actions of one or
more players preceding the random event.

Settlers is a good example. Is a roll of 6 good for me, or bad for
me? Well, that depends on where I built, where the robber is, who
else is on the 6, how much of a threat they are, how much they need
the resource they produce on 6, who has what ports, etc.

There is certainly randomness in Settlers, and it certainly influences
the outcome of games. But the interaction between random and
nonrandom events is more complex than you describe above, so it's not
so simple to analyze the game.

Also, your assumption that there is only one best leaf and only one
path to it is true for approximately no games. Almost all games have
a large number of leaves marked "I win", a large number marked "Fred
wins", sometimes some marked "Draw", etc. (and more, if there are more
than 2 players). Reaching any winning leaf is just as good as any
other winning leaf.
