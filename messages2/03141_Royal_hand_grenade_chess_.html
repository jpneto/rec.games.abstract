Hand-grenade variants are those where pieces may move only to an empty square,
but thereby capture all enemy pieces adjacent to that square, diagonally or
orthogonally; (i.e. up to 8 captures per turn!) They tend to be fairly quick.

"Royal" variants are those where the winner of the game is whoever captures
the opposition king first, as opposed to (say) "all-off" or "misere" versions.
Standard chess is almost royal. (But no stalemate rule in royal chess variants.)

So in Royal hand-grenade, all pieces move as in normal chess, but only to empty
squares, capturing adjacents; first to capture king wins. Castling is allowed,
merely provided empty squares between and neither piece has moved; ("check" is
ignored). Pawns may start with single or double move as normal, (en passant
cannot apply), and they promote to Q,R,B or N at discretion.

-----

Royal hand-grenade is fascinating. We have been playing it here in coffee
breaks for a little while. It's fun. The balance between getting big pieces
out, and keeping the king protected, is delicate. Games can be on a knife-edge
for quite a while till suddenly all hell breaks loose. Games tend to be a
lot shorter than standard chess, but full of subtleties nonetheless. We have
only once got to an "end-game", but that may reflect our lack of ability
rather than the game itseld.

The value of pieces seems about the same as in ordinary chess, except bishops
are much stronger - even slightly better than rooks.
