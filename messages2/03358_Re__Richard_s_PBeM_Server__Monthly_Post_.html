There's a much more important principle:

Under the Berne Convention, a game cannot be copyrighted. Period.

The *particular expression of the rules of a game* can be copyrighted,
as can the *particular likeness of the components*. But the game
itself cannot.

And since the rules of Othello can be easily restated in any number of
ways, and the components are no more than coins and a checkerboard,
there is little or no protection on Othello even disregrading the fact
that the game was inventing in the XIX century.
