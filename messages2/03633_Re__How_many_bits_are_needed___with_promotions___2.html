You could even use the 7th type of piece as "special piece". If it is a
King start square or a rook start square, then it is an unmoved King or
Rook, so you still have an eighth type spare. And there is infact no need
to mark the king. Only mark a rook as suitable for castling if neither
king nor rook has moved.

> You can encode en passant in three bits. If the last move played
> was advancing a pawn two squares, then use those three bits
> to represent the file that the pawn is on. If the last move was
> not moving a pawn two squares forward, then use those three bits
> to represent a file where a pawn of the appropriate color is not
> on the appropriate rank.

Or you can save another three bits, by saying a special piece on the 4th
rank is a pawn which can be taken en-passant.

Finally, you need to say who's turn it is. You could use another bit, or
you could say that a special piece anywhere other than 4th rank or King or
Rook starting square, is no piece at all, but indicates that the player
who's turn it is next - white square = white, black square = black. In the
unlikely event that all suitable squares have a piece on, then the lack of
any special non-piece indicates that whichever set of squares are totally
covered is who's turn it is.

So the whole position is now down to a max of 192 bits, including
castling, en-passent and who's turn it is. Of course it could be less if
there are less than 32 pieces still on the board. You could also encode
the piece list, as only 7 of 8 combinations are used for each piece. I
reckon that will go into 90 bits if packed, rather than the 96 unpacked
(log(7^32) / log(2)). That gives 186 bits.
