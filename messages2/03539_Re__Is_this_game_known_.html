>>I have heard it called "tug of war", and also "mental ju-jitsu"

>One of the neat features of this game is that it can handle more
>players on an appropriately shaped board--admittedly, the north-south
>pair on a square board are playing a completely separate game from the
>east-west team, but a six-player hexagonal game strikes me as
>interestingly complex.

The NS pair is not _completely_ separate from the EW pair: You do have
to consider speeding up your tugging if the token is nearing an edge
perpendicular to your own. But I agree that the hexagonal game might
be more interesting.

Note that the hexagonal board can be used with three players as well
as six: You just place players at every other edge, so no player is
directly opposite of another. You can also play four players on the
hexagonal board: You place two players adjacent to each other and the
other two opposite these. This will give neighbouring players an
incentive to cooperate, but only up to a point. It is probably more
interesting than the square-board tug-of-war game. With five players,
it will probably be too much of an advantage for the player with no
directly opposing player, but that may be compensated for by the fact
that the otehrs realize this and cooperate. Alternatively, that player
can get less "money" for his biddings. How much less is difficult to
say.
