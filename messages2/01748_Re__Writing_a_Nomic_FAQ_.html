I played a variant called quasi-Mao, which as I understand it was essentially
the same, only with a different rule subset. No rules are told to the
initiate. He merely... plays. If he misplays (ie plays an illegal card,
doesn't do something he's supposed to, do something he's not, etc.) he is given
an n-card penalty. By observation he can figure out some rules, and can then
check. Obviously, having gained so many cards for screwing up, he's not going
to win for a while. Eventually, however, he'll learn the rules, and will be
able to win and create his own. In my variant of quasi-Mao, there were at
least 20 rules, and possibly as many as 30. (It's probably grown since then,
but...) Mao itself has many thousands of rules. Of course, noone _knows_ them
all, but if you play with a strange group you can enforce your rules and they
enforce theirs. Makes for a very interesting game.
