> Nim is certainly interesting study in its own right, but it belongs to
> combinatorial games (games that are analyzable by particular mathematical
> methods) as a small and rather dull subsets of all fun abstract games.

You need to read more about its usefulness. Combinatorial game theory
can be used in games that are generally considered to be interesting
(e.g. Go) or games that more people would find interesting (dots and
boxes) if they knew enough the strategy to not just play randomly.

The theory itself is also interesting. People interested in that may
be more interested in reading "On Numbers and Games" than something
like "Winning Ways", "Mathematical Go", "The Dots and Boxes Game" or
"Games of No Chance".
