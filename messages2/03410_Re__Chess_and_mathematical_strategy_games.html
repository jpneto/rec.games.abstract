Well until the computer is able to detect and fix bugs in
the *human* program, it is always safe to say that 'man is better'.
And if you're not going to base your judgements on the *outcome* of
the contest alone, including all *3* wins of Kasparov, then you
leave *your* position open to dismissive arguments when the outcome
(some day) favors the machine -- "Yes, the machine did win, but
obviously the human player just had a slight (viral?) bug, so the
machine is not really the better player."

>Since the seventies, the best computer chess program has *ALWAYS* been

Well you certainly cannot call using the book openings "based on brute
force" (maybe "brute knowledge", but notice how little good that does
when not used). You are arguing out of two sides of your mouth to say
the program's advantage comes from using brute force and yet claiming
that the program lost (in Kasparov's case) because it was forced to
use brute force (taken out of its book).

I'll give 10:1 odds that Kasparov wins again in '97 (unless
he has a bug :-)
