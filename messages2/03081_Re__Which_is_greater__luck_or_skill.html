Daniel Elliott Loeb writes:
>Dick King writes:

>A mathematician (Fischer I believe) developed a system in which
>a certain bid means that I have exactly one of two certain small cards
>(say the 2 and 3 of clubs).
>Your partner's reply indicates if he has the other.
>In that case, you and your partner know which of you has the 2 of clubs
>and which has the 3 of clubs. However, the opponents only know that
>you both have one of the two cards.

>After this system was first used it was outlawed in tournament play.

Why?

I seriously doubt that this system would be playable but it is legal
to require that an opening bid or response includes certain specific
cards and vary your responses based on the interferences that can
drawn from this requirement.
