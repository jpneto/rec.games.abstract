Card game statistics problem

From: oauld@convex.com (Orion Auld)
Date: 26 04 1996 00:00:00 GMT


My friends and I are having an argument about a card game. In this card
game, each player has their own deck, and draws cards and discards cards
from their deck in a manner similar to a traditional card game. If a
player pays a certain amount of resources, that player may force the
other player to remove two cards from their draw pile and place them on
the discard pile.

One strategy that is available to the players is to try to run the other
player out of cards using this power. But this requires significant
devotion of resources to achieve. Some people, however, use this ability
at random, or according to some method that is not designed to run
the other player out of cards, thinking that removing cards from the
draw pile is somehow advantageous to them, even if the game ends one
way or another before their opponent runs out of cards.

My questions are these:

1) Would randomly using this ability affect the ability of the target player
to achieve certain combinations of cards?

2) Assuming that you can see every card that is on the table and in the
discard pile, is their any method of calculating or counting that would
give the player using this ability some foresight which would make it
advantageous to use it?
