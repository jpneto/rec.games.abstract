> Here is the list of the variants:
> 1. Kamakazi Chess: Piece that captures an opponent's piece is also
> removed from the game. Based on this variant, a king couldn't
> capture a piece or it would lose the game.

Reminds me of the very fun "nuclear chess". When you take a piece there
is an "explosion" at that square, killing everything adjacent to that
square. (ie 9 squares are affected.) When you first try this White wins
fast because of immediate mate/material threats. (The fastest of which
is 1.Nf3 e6 2.Ne5++ (Your attacks on d7 and f7 both check the king.))
After a bit it becomes more even.

It has 2 variants, one lets the king take without explosions. (Avoids a
lot of silly mates, for instance if your king is on e8, your bishop on
f8 is trapped, you have a piece on d6, and I have a bishop on e7, then
that is mate. Also the Q can mate the K by herself.) The other lets the
attacking player choose whether or not there is an explosion. (Allows
takes in a lot of situations.)

Another idea that can be used in regular chess and in nuclear chess is
giving people the choice between 2 pawn moves or one regular move.

> 2. "Eye for an Eye" Chess: If you capture an opponent's piece, you
> also remove the same type of piece from your side. For example, if you
> capture an opponent's bishop, you also remove one of your bishops from
> the board.
> Promotion disrupts the "symatry" of this variant. If you promote
> a pawn to gain a piece, your opponent doesn't also gain that piece.
> Promotion can also bring back a piece that one's opponent doesn't
> have. If your opponent captured your newly brought back queen, for
> example, they wouldn't have to remove any of their pieces from the
> board, if they didn't have the queen.

Looks like it would be too even for my tastes.

> 3. "I feel your pain" Chess: Like "Eye for an Eye", except, you choose
> which type of piece you want to remove. For example, if you capture
> an opponent's rook, you don't need to remove one of your rooks. You
> could, instead, remove one of your pawns from the game.
>
This is more workable. (Nice way to develop.) But I will stick with
nuclear and bughouse for silly chess.
