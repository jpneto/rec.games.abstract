Don Woods has made a good point about how i worded the rules.
He noted that i hadnt specifically described how you may play
the Zoid your opponent gives you. Here is a clarification and
and whole restatement of the rules.

ZOIDS

(c)1995 Nathan A.Smith

========================================================================

Abstract:

ZOIDS was designed to be a pure-skill two-player
abstract board game with no apparent advantage to
either the first or second player. The name comes
from the word "zoid", or "animal-like thing".


========================================================================

Rules:

1. The playing surface is a connected grid of all the edges of
some equal-sized regular hexagons packed together.

2. A Zoid consists of one or more hexagon edges connected at
the vertices of the playing surface. In order to be a NEW
Zoid, it cannot be rotated and/or flipped over into the same
form of a Zoid already played.

3. The game begins with the last player giving the first player
the first Zoid of the game.

4. Play of turn consists of 2 steps:

a. play the given Zoid somewhere upon a completely
unoccupied portion of the playing surface.

b. give the next player a new Zoid that can be legally
played somewhere, unless no new Zoid can fit. You
=====> may rotate it and turn it over anyway you like as long
=====> as it fits into empty space on the playing surface.

5. The game ends when no new Zoid can be legally played.

6. The first player unable to give the next player a new Zoid
instead gives that player a half point.

7. Each hexagon touched only by one player's Zoids scores one
point for that player.

8. The highest combined allied point total wins.


Note: Rule 6 is a defect in the logical structure of these rules
and has no other purpose than to prevent tie games. If you
wish to allow ties, then Rule 6 can be dropped and Rules 7&8
combined together into one statement, making 6 rules in all.

=======================================================================
