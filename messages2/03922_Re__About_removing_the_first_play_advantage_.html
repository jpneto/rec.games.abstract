gerryq@indigo.ie (Gerry Quinn) writes:
> That makes me wonder - what happens an abstract game if, at any time,
> and once only, one player may swap sides? What happens chess? Has
> anyone tried this?

I assume you mean that, once during the game, either player may insist
that the two players swap. If _each_ player can do so once, I think it
would have almost no effect, since whenever one player ordered the swap
the other player could use his swap to undo it, so the result would be
unchanged except when the first swap was in error (at least, in the
judgment of the other player).

If only one swap total is permitted, but either player may call for it,
then you are in effect playing "misere", i.e., playing to "lose" based
on the rules of the original game. E.g., in chess, you would be trying
to force the other player to mate you, because if you could do that you
could swap sides just before the mate and thereby win.

The swap version is not quite the same as misere, since you don't have
to achieve the full victory condition. If you can force the other player
to achieve a substantial lead, you can then swap and now you're playing
the normal game but with a lead. In true misere, your opponent would be
trying the whole time to avoid winning, but in a "swap" game you control
his moves after the swap and can play to win.

Unfortunately, it is probably fairly easy (in some games) to force your
opponent to have a temporary lead. E.g., in chess, you might manage to
shove your queen up next to the opponent's king, such that the only way
to get out of the check is to capture the queen. So as soon as you move
the queen there, you swap. Hm, actually, it's even easier than that,
since once you swap it doesn't matter if the queen-capture was forced.
So just get your queen out into the open, shove the queen up to where it
can be captured, then swap, capture the queen, and play on to win.

You can limit some of these abuses by saying, for instance, that a player
can make the swap only when it is about to be his move, i.e., the other
player then gets to make the first move in the swapped position. But
that would still be open to things like the forced queen capture scenario.

Anyway, the main point is that allowing a swap at an arbitrary point later
in the game, changes the goal structure of the game dramatically. It is
in no way comparable to the "equalisation" methods that have been under
discussion in this thread.
