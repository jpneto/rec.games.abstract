Quoting marksteere@gmail.com's message of Yesterday:

> Checkers, Chess, and Go are all magnificent achievements for their
> simplicity and fundamental uniqueness. Checkers especially has
> probably been more imitated than any other game. How many games
> include the Checkers jump? The jump is a wonderful mechanism. It's
> simple, unique, and easy to perform. But every time I see the jump
> appear in a new game I want to barf. Designers: Just leave the jump in
> Checkers and come up with your own mechanisms. It's challenging but
> not impossible.

I think rather differently where mechanisms are concerned.

Some mechanisms in new games appear contrived to me. I can't think of
an example here and now, but at least one of Sid Sackson's games struck
me this way.

In contrast I find some of the older mechanisms beautiful in their
simplicity, and some games still find elegant ways to use them. I like
movement to adjacent squares, or by sliding in a given direction;
I like capture by jump, replacement or surrounding.

I don't dislike strange new mechanisms altogether. Cannon is a new game
that appeals to me, though it uses non-traditional mechanisms like the
cannon firing, and the movement of a whole column that I've seen in
another modern game (Epaminondas?), in addition to more traditional
forms of movement and capture.
