This is a true story, it's a bit long to tell but I think it makes an
interresting math puzzle, for which I couldn't figure out a solution (but I'm
not good at math). Here it goes:

My brother created a modern art association with 6 friends of him. Every now
and then (say each month for instance) they buy an artwork from a young
artist (a painting, a statue, etc). This artwork belong to the association and
circles between members: everyone can keep it during one month, then passes it
to the next guy.

Now they would like to include an 8th person in their association, but the
president being very conservative will not allow it unless he can be provided
with a fair way to swap artworks between 8 persons. He has 3 main criterion
for fairness:

A) The artwork should go to everyone once, before somebody can have it for
the second time.

B) There should not be too many discrepancies between the number of artworks
curently own by the members. It's OK that somebody has 1 and somebody else has
none (it's always the case if there are less than 8 artworks) but it is not
allowed that somebody has 2 and someone else none.

C) The artworks should not travel always the same way. In other words, there
should not be a guy who always gets the newly acquired artwork, whereas
another guy must always wait 7 months before he can have it.

Now my questions:
1) Is it possible to design a swapping strategy to meet the 3 criterions ?
2) If no, can you demonstrate that it's impossible ?
3) If it's impossible with 8 persons, would it be better with 7 or 9 ?

Below are a few trials I made. Colums represent months, row are artworks and
numbers 1 through 8 are the members of the association. (NB Don't use a
proportional font to display these, as numbers should align vertically).

.......12345678
This satisfies A and B, but violates C (person 1 gets all artworks first,
person 8 gets them last).

.....34567812

.......78123456
This is slighly more fair, but still violates C (persons 1,3,5 and 7 get new
artworks once in four, whereas 2,4,6 and 8 never do)

Trial 3:

......34567812

This finally satisfies C, as well as A, but now B is violated (e.g. during the
8th month, person 8,6 and 4 get two artworks, whereas 3,5 and 7 have none)

Trial 4:

This agains violates B (during the 8th month person 1 gets 2 artworks, and
person 5 has none).

I did many more trials, but could not come with a satisfactory solution.
