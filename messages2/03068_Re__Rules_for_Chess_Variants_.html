> With some help, I worked out some bugs with Hexagonal Chess. It's like
> chess, plus 50%. Makes sense, eh?
> You create a hex board, much like boards used for the game of Hex:
> parrellogram in shape, 9 by 9. 9x9 because you have three colors of hexes,
> ergo three bishops. We tried to maintain as close a resemblance to basic
> chess as possible, but some things were clearly impossible from the
> beginning. Bishops and queens were bothersome at first. Consider how
> bishops slide beteen two pieces that are diagonally adjecent. The bishop
> slide between them with not so much as a by-your-leave. Well, we wanted to
> have that same element, but with the opening board positions we tried,
> you'd get first turn captures! Gads. So, we modified it.
> _ _ _
> / \ _ / \ _ / R \
>
> \ _ / \ _ / p1\ _ /
> / \ _ / \ _ / p2\
> \ _ / \ _ / B \ _ /
> \ _ / \ _ /
>
> The bishop here would have been able to slip past the two pawns and tag
> the rook. Well, we said that one of the pawns would have to be gone in
> order for the move/capture to be made by the bishop. Likewise for queens.
> Rooks move in three directions, and bishops move along their color in big
> steps, i.e. not exactly touching the next hex, but connected by a line.
> Knights moved out 2, over one, as usual, but, since there are 3 primary
> straight ways to go, over would have been 2 different places, so it'd have
> to be out 2, out-over 1 (versus out 2, back-out 1). Opening position was
>
> _________
> \RNBKBQBNR\
> \PPPPPPPPP\
> \ \
> \ \
> \ \
> \ \
> \ \
> \PPPPPPPPP\
> \RNBQBKBNR\
> ~~~~~~~~~~
>
> I know this got tedious, but figuring these details out took 2 hours of
> debate and play testing, and it is a pretty fun variant.

First time I have heard of mention of hexagonal chess on the Internet.
Did you know that there is an established variation of hexagonal chess
played on hexagonal shaped board of hexagons. W Glinski invented the
game in the 1950's and there were some clubs established and some books
printed. "First Theories of Hexagonal Chess" 1974 and "Rules of Hexagonal
Chess with examples of first openings." both by the inventor. Mr Glinski
died recently so it is hard to buy these books at the moment, but I believe
that his widow is considering having them reprinted.
