> >Hello all. Has anyone considered playing a game like so:
> >
> >Two players, Alice and Bob, are playing a game such as chess, checkers,
> >go, etc. But they make their moves "simultaneously:" For any given
> >board position, Alice writes down her move on a sheet of paper
> >(momentarily keeping it secret from Bob), and Bob does the same at the
> >same time.
> >
> >Once Alice and Bob have commited to their moves in this manner, Alice
> >tells Bob her move and Bob tells Alice his; the moves are played out.
> >
> >If there is contention (e.g. in go, Alice and Bob place a stone in the
> >same location) then a fair coin is tossed to determine who actually gets
> >to place the stone, and the loser gets a free move.
> >
> >I haven't thought this out too much, but I think that it might
> >interestingly change the dynamic for some games, such as Hex, where the
> >first player generally has the advantage. (In the simultaneous version,
> >there is NO "first" player.)
> >
> >Anyone have any comments?

For games where turns involve piece movement, it might be interesting to
remove the conflict from the rules by treating moves as follows:
1) Remove pieces moved from the board simultaneously.
2) Place them where they were moved to simultaneously. If more than one
piece moves to the same cell, they coexist on that cell until one of
them moves again. (In chess, this would mean that the pieces would be
uncapturable until one of them moves, since you cannot move to a square
containing your own piece.

This does not fix all problems in chess, where you still have the
problem that a player can be illegally left in check, (but as long as
you are mangling the game you can make the object capturing the king
without too much additional damage.) It ought to work fairly well for
lines of action.

In general, I prefer fixing the rules to be allow any moves to be
simultaneous without there being a conflict, without involving
stochastic elements. (Of course the resulting game may bear less
resemblence to the original this way.)
