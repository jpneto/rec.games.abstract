<< Has anyone also experimented with rules variants, such as allowing any
number of marbles to push any smaller number, and/or allowing the distance of
the push to be as great as the difference between the two sets (i.e., three can
push one two spaces, etc.), or perhaps even any number can push any equal or
smaller number, with no restoring of the position on the next turn? I like the
general concept of Abalone, and I love the physical feel of the board and
marbles, but as the rules stand, I think the game is far too defensive and
slow-developing. >>

I've tried to find a "fix" for Abalone, but changes in the rules either give
one player too much advantage, or make it too difficult to manuever. A number
of marbles pushing an equal number of marbles, at some point gets pushed back,
and nothing is gained; however, at the edge of the board, it leaves one player
defenseless. Abalone's charm is in its simplicity. The new starting position
is the first change I've seen that doesn't interfere with the basic simplicity
of the game.

The comparison with Sumo wrestling wouldn't allow a player to self-divide into
two parts, but whatever tricks a Sumo wrestler uses aren't adapted into Abalone
either.
