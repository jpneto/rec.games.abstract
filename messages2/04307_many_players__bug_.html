Something bugs me about multi-player games. When I play the same
game with the same friends many many times, on one hand the game
keeps getting interesting in several ways, as a two-player game
would, but on the other hand we get to know each other's overall
strenght quite well and we naturally counter each other
proportionally to it. Our match outcomes gets balanced then, each
of us roughly winning as often as the other. My trouble is, while
we keep struggling to gain more control over the game, over
strategy and each other's psychology, that control eludes us and
the game outcome gets chaotic and anyway only a matter of who is
going to win this time instead of the next time. And we hardly
feel responsible for our victories.

Playing with new people is more a denial than a satisfactory
answer, although not knowing your opponents' tricks and overall
strength makes a good part of a game, of its fun and strategical
depths. This is not 'all' any multi-player game turns out to be,
is it?

Don't you feel like me? It must be that victories stop mattering
in games with more than two players. Very much like in life, it
seems that playing well, getting better, ... have got no clear
measure. You decide your goal, what you enjoy, etc.

Well, I would keep playing to study that exotic bug.
