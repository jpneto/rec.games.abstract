>A) The artwork should go to everyone once, before somebody can have it for
>the second time.

>B) There should not be too many discrepancies between the number of artworks
>curently own by the members. It's OK that somebody has 1 and somebody else has
>none (it's always the case if there are less than 8 artworks) but it is not
>allowed that somebody has 2 and someone else none.

>C) The artworks should not travel always the same way. In other words, there
>should not be a guy who always gets the newly acquired artwork, whereas
>another guy must always wait 7 months before he can have it.

I'd suggest this method. Pick a number k between 1 and the number
of people you have (inclusive). Every time a new painting becomes
available, decrement k (if k ends up as 0 then return it to n).
Give the new painting to the person numbered closest to k (without
going over) who is in the group of people with the fewest paintings.

So... starting with three people and zero paintings and k==2
(gwfp == group with fewest paintings)

k=2 1 2 3 gwfp=1 2 3

add painting a
k=1 1a 2 3 gwfp=2 3

pass paintings along
k=1 1 2a 3 gwfp=1 3

pass paintings along
k=1 1 2 3a gwfp=1 2

add new painting b
k=3 1 2b 3a gwfp=1

Of course, your criteria C isn't quite satisfied if always passing
person m's paintings on to person m+1 is 'traveling the same way'.
But, this satisfies B and distributes the starting point of paintings
which is the latter half of C's requirement. And it easily satisfies A.

The major disadvantage to always passing left is that once painting
'd' enters the picture, it's always going be shipped around with
the same other set of paintings. This is nice from a logistics
viewpoint (I only have to deliver all of the paintings I have
to Joe at the end of each month.... never one to Mary in Wisconsin,
one to Joe, and three to Louis in Salt Lake City). But, permutation
would give the gallery life.

So, here's another suggestion. At then end of each month, randomly
reassign the paintings. Preserving B. I'd suggest this (being
the geek that I am):

Assign each painting a sequence number and a Fibonacci number
(should probably give it two Fibonacci numbers so that each
maintains its state).

Now, at the end of each month, assume no one has any paintings.
With each painting (in sequence order), calculate its
next Fibonacci number. Mod this number with the number
of people in the gfwp. Assign this painting to that
person.

Any uniform random number generation technique would work, but
Fibonacci has the appeal for me from a simplicity standpoint.

This doesn't satisfy A completely, but it doesn't do too badly at
it. It does satisfy B completely, and it does a kickin' job on C.

As odd as this may seem, I think another good newsgroup for this
question would be rec.games.juggling. I think there may be a
nifty parallel to site-swap theory.
