I would like to develop a short class on "Introduction to
Strategy Gaming" to teach at a community college. My intent
is to focus this class on face-to-face, non-computer games
of strategy (i.e. primarily covering, but not limited to,
"war games"). I would like this to be an all-inclusive
overview, targeted to non-gamers or new gamers who would
like to know a little bit about all of the options out there:
board games, miniature games, roleplaying games, and (collectible
and non-collectible) card games. I want to talk some about
history of games (where they came from and why they are how
they are), basic game and gameplay concepts and components,
characteristics of "good" games, etc.

The class will most likely be about 4-5 evening sessions, each
with a mixture of some lecture followed by a chance to sample
a couple of games that illustrate the points discussed.

I'm interested in anything you have to say about this:
- Is this a good idea? Would anyone actually attend a
class like this?
- Have you ever known anyone who designed a class like
this? How did they handle it?
- Can you recommend any good resources (on the web or elsewhere)
for high-level overview information of games, game
history, and general strategy game information?


Please let me know,
