Now that you've all been thinking about the strategy for various
abstract games, I would like to draw your attention to my favourite
game: Hex. David Bush mentioned my Hex playing program Queenbee
(www.cs.ualberta.ca/~queenbee); I'll also refer you to Vadim
Anshelevich's very strong program Hexy (home.earthlink.net/~vanshel/)
and the Hex discussion group at clubs.yahoo.com/clubs/maitregshexclub .

Particularly, notice the message that I posted on the Hex discussion
group on November 1st. I'd like to repeat part of that message here:

> There is very little literature on Hex, but that will hopefully
> change. Together with graph theorist dr. Ryan Hayward
> (www.cs.ualberta.ca/~hayward) I am planning to write a book on Hex
> next year. The book will basically contain information on the rules,
> history, strategy, tactics, and mathematical background of Hex.
>
> Ryan Hayward would like it to be small and consise, while I would
> like it to contain *everything* known to Earthlings about Hex. It
> looks as though these two things are not at all incompatible, though.
> Your input is more than welcome; what I would especially love to see
> is annotated games between top players that illustrate advanced
> strategic or tactical concepts.

Note that everyone will, of course, get due credit in our book. (:
