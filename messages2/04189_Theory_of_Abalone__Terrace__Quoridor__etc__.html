I am looking for recent pure strategy board games for which there exists
some theory. Let me clarify some of these terms to explain what exactly
I'm looking for.

When I say "theory" I include mathematical analysis but do not restrict
myself to this. "Theory" in the chess sense---advanced strategic concepts,
opening books, solved endgames, annotated games by strong players---counts.

As for "recent board games," I give three examples in the subject line.
The intent is to exclude (on the one hand) classic games like go and chess
and checkers whose literature is too overwhelmingly vast, and (on the other
hand) games that are known only to specialists and are not played by the
general public (e.g., many of the games described in _Winning_Ways_).

"Pure strategy" may be too strong; games like bridge, Scrabble, and
backgammon might squeak in, although I would exclude bridge as being too
"classic." (Backgammon is an old game but I might include it since I
believe the literature is not so large.)

Finally, here are two specific examples that illustrate what I am hoping
for. One is a paper that gives an optimal Mastermind strategy, in the
sense of minimizing the expected number of guesses in the standard game
with four spaces and six colors. The second is some literature I found
by the U.S. Othello Association on Othello. Othello borders on being too
"classic" but I include it because its literature (at least in English)
is of manageable size.
