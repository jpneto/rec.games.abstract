Good work. The non-drawing mechanism is cunning - it applies
all throughout the game, which is more natural, and also solves
the problem of who gets 2 moves when the max-group numbers
are equal.... as they are NEVER equal! Cute.

> The game isn't quite complete yet, because it's not clear whether the
> leader and his opponent should play 1 and 2 stones, respectively, on
> their turns, or 2 and 3 stones.

As always, the answer may be different for different contexts.

For over-the-board play 1&2 should be quite sufficient.
But for email games, players often want to speed things up,
and may elect for 2&3. However I suspect that 1&2 would still be
sufficiently racy.

> you feel as though you're playing an asymmetrical game due
> the gross differences in power between the two players at any moment.

Good point! I played a few games against myself, and even on micro-
boards
it has that feeling. I played several on a 3-per-side hex board, and
first-mover seemed to win them all, though even there strategic
variations
loomed up. On the 5-per-side I'm guessing mucho strategic concerns.

If anyone wants to check (and maybe correct) my results for the ultra-
tiny
2-per-side board, I make it First wins if he starts in the centre,
and Second wins otherwise.

BTW Nick - for the square-board game, did you envisage diagonals
as being connected, or not? I would guess not, as otherwise
almost everything would be connectable too easily.
