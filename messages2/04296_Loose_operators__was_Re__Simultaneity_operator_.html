>This generalization has the problem that there may be several ways to
>remove moves such that there is no stand-off. In the Chinese checkers
>example, removing any one of the two conflicting moves will make the
>rest commute, but there is no fair way to choose which should be
>removed.

\begin{silly}

Why not allow for operators that do not map game A onto game B
perfectly? Call them "loose operators" -- the term can apply equally
well to the players. :-)

Loose operators would permit the original definition of the
Simultaneity operator, as well as

* the Drinking operator -- players drink when they lose a point, a
piece, or whatever

* the Strip operator -- players remove an article of clothing when
they lose a point, a piece, or whatever

* the Semi-Demiurgic operator -- players can propose a rule change on
their turn. If the rule change is voted down, players take a penalty
(see the Drinking operator and the Strip operator)... (Thanks to
Chris Dickson for this one.)

\end{silly}

Any more ideas for loose operators?
