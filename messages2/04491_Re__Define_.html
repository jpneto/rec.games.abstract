On Fri, 3 Nov 2000, Allan Crossman wrote:

> Nathan Sanders <sanders@ling.ucsc.edu> wrote:
>
> > Only games in which both players make their initial moves simultaneously
> > can hope to be competely luck-free; otherwise the very process of deciding
> > that someone goes first puts some amount of luck (no matter how small)
> > into the game.
>
> Surely if both players make their first move at the same time without
> seeing the other's move, the result is as much determined by luck as in
> "Paper, Scissors, Stone"?

Agreed. Note my phrasing above: in order to have any hope of being
luck-free, the players' initial moves must be simultaneous. This doesn't
imply that all games with simultaneous first moves are luck-free. As a
trivial example, consider a game in which the players choose their
initial positions in secret, and roll dice from there on. Certainly not
luck-free.
