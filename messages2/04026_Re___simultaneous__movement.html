Alexandre Muniz <munizao@cyberhighway.net> writes:

|> In (some) games where the only moves are piece placement, (go, hex,
|> go-moku, &c,) the conflict can be resolved by creating a new neutral
|> piece type that is placed at the position where two players
|> simultaneously move.

Interesting idea. It's similar to, but not quite the same as,
the alternate rule that: if both try to place at the same point,
that point becomes forbidden to place at forever, (not just for
the remainder of that turn). In gomoku and hex it is the same as this;
in go, not quite, as the forbidden space would become a permanent liberty.


|> Go-moku might work fairly well with a similar rule.

Yes, I think it would. The games would go on longer, but eventually a win
would become certain once they'd sprawled about enough.
(This assumes simulataneous 5's cancel out and mean play on.)


|> For Hex I might prefer that a simultaneous move position should be
|> counted towards connections for both sides.

I suspect that either way it wouldn't be so hot for Hex. Hex tends to
be pretty tight, so the presence of either a universal blocker or a
universal connecter would tend to produce a lot of draws, I guess,
either by impossibilty or by simultaneous success.

In go I suspect it would also be bad, as a vulnerable group (say with
a row of three spaces in it) would only need one dual-hit to become
permanently invulnerable, without the owner even having to waste a move
on it! Hardly seems fair. Come to think of it, this would even apply
to DEAD groups with a single eye of more than one space - if the owner
insists that the other actually remove it (e.g. with area rules) it gets
at least a 50% chance of becoming invulnerable! Playable, but bizarre.


Simultaneous moves should be VERY good for backgammon too - for which there's
an easy way to resolve conflicts - just re-roll the dice! It would be
a good way of reducing the grosser vagaries of luck, (though by no means
all the vagaries of it), and also remove the 1st-move advantage, as always.

I like it!
