Actually, they've been around for a while.

The game I consider to be the ur-game of this type is described in
Dave Parlett's _A Dictionary of Card Games_ under the title _GOPS_
("Game of Pure Strategy") and is basically identical to what was
described as PJJ. Parlett does not know the inventor nor the date,
although I got the impression that is from the 1960's or 1970's.

_Raj_ is a recent reprint of the German game _Hol's de Geier_, which
was released in 1987 or 1988. It was invented by Alex Randolph, but he
claims it was based on a game he saw played by Indian members of the
British army in the 1940's. Raj is basically identical to GOPS except
that, as Treesong pointed out, there are negative score cards as well
as positive; also, it has a very clever tiebreaker rule.

_High Society_, by Reiner Knizia, uses similar equipment but does not
feature simultaneous bidding. Otherwise, it is substantially like
Raj/Hol's, but the sequential bidding makes a tremendous difference in
play.

_Pico_, which is a 1996 release from Frank Nestel and Doris Matthaus,
is similar but with a much smaller deck. Players complete with a hand
of five cards drawn randomly from an eleven-card deck; unlike the
other games described above, the cards one plays are also the cards
one hopes to win, rather than a separate deck.

We played one other similar game, Alan Parr's _Dirty Dozen_. The main
elaborations on the basic GOPS design were: There are positive and
negative scoring tiles (+30 points, halve your score, etc.) and a
second deck which determined how the winner of each trick would be
determined (highest card, lowest card, second highest, etc.). In each
turn, a scoring card and a card indicating how the card would be won
would be revealed.

Pretty much everyone involved in these games agreed that _High
Society_ was the best of the lot. However, _Hol's__Raj_ is very highly
regarded as well, and I found _Pico_ to have a quirky charm.

The first game to which I heard the title "Pschological Jui-jitsu"
applied was actually a two-player board game. On a board of seven
spaces, place a token in the center. Each player begins with a store
of 50 points. Each turn, the players simultaneously reveal a bid of
some portion of his remaining points. The higher bidder moves the
token one space towards the opponent. Both players lose the points
bid, and the next turn begins. The first player to move the token to
the far end of the board wins.

(Knizia's game _Tor_ is very similar to this, but the bidding units
are cards from 1-13, with the special feature that a 13 loses to any
card ranked 1-10.)

>Finally, I've forgotten what King's Court was but I seem to recall it was
>also of this type.

King's Court is an overly-elaborate Crazy Eights (Uno) variant.
