Geenius at Wrok <geenius@enteract.com> writes:

>On 24 Mar 2000, Ron Hale-Evans wrote:

>> 1. One new category of game is the kind that centers around breaking
>> its rules, like Cosmic Encounter, Magic: the Gathering, and
>> Zarcana. Is there an accepted name for these? (If so, where did it
>> come from?) I have considered "exceptions games", "exceptions-based
>> games", and "rule-breaking games", but these are all clunky. And can
>> you name any more games in this category?

>They are clunky, but more to the point, they're not particularly correct.
>Whatever they may like to say about themselves, Cosmic Encounter, Zarcana
>et al. do not "break their rules." Rather, certain players have certain
>powers that trump the limitations other players are subject to. Not only
>is overriding a rule not the same thing as breaking it, the mechanism that
>allows this trumping is itself a rule. You can't have games, especially
>complex ones, without a set of hard and fast rules, even if all of those
>rules don't apply to all players all the time.

Hmm. I'll point you to the same site I just pointed Chris Dickson:
_The Life of Games_, <http://www.gamepuzzles.com/tlog1.htm>. The first
article, "Unwritten Rules", makes a pretty good case that virtually
every game has a bunch of unwritten, unstatable rules that are very
far from hard and fast. I don't know whether you could play a game
that consisted *entirely* of implicit rules of that sort... It's
interesting to think about.

Mayfair billed Cosmic as "the game that breaks its own rules". I
really don't want to become entangled in a semantic argument, than
which there are few things more pointless, but why can't you say that
Cosmic has rules of Order 2 that let players break rules of Order 1?
This kind of thing goes on in Nomic all the time.

>That being said, I would focus on the differences between players as being
>special powers or privileges as opposed to "exceptions." (Cf. Illuminati,
>in which each secret society has its own unique victory condition.) Thus
>you might call this genre "unique-power games."

That's another good shot, but I think I'll keep looking. Cosmic
Encounter is not a unique-power game, because many powers, such as
Insect and Plant, let the player mimic other players' powers. And in
MtG, many players can have the same card.

Anyway, I find the word "exception" even less objectionable than
"rule-breaking". Many games have rules with exceptions: every piece in
Chess can be captured *except* the king, for example. Cosmic Encounter
plays *with* exceptions rather than *under* them.

>As for the rest, I think others have already referred to nomic with the
>term "metagame," and Fluxx clearly falls into the same category.

Well, I didn't say I coined it. The first time I heard the term
applied to Nomic was around 1986, when my friends and I put up posters
advertising the Y Nomic Club at Yale. The name of the club was
mistakenly reported in a Bitnet e-zine as the Yale Metagaming Society,
because our posters featured a repeating motif of the word METAGAME in
large, excitingly chunky letters. I don't know who coined it locally;
it was definitely one of four people.

Yes, I consider Fluxx a metagame.
