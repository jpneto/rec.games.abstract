i think you were thinking about abstract games that were
*solved* and thus all the information from start to finish
was known. every position was solved, every subsequent
course of action was known.

the usual use of the term "perfect information" here is a
more of a reference to the starting conditions and the course
of play - you see the entire playing environment, there are no
cards face down, no secretive wall preventing you from seeing
any information, no random roll of dice to determine anything
in the course of a turn.


the only thing that isnt perfect in this sense is in the thinking
process of the players, who are only bound by their own wetware
shortcomings.
