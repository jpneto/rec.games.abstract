There is no equivalent of that in Stackers, as you don't win by
capturing opponent pieces, so you don't get this kind of stalemate.

The closest I can see is a situation where there are one piece of each
colour next to each other. One jumps on top of the other to block it,
but in the next round, it has to move away, and the other can jump on
top of that to block it, reversing the situation. This can potentially
go on forever, but dependong on which edge is closest, one of the
players can win by breaking the pattern and make for the edge. Since
you cannot pass, you cannot block forever, and once you unblock, the
other can run away.

> Nobody has to force a draw. One way a draw can be reached is when
> both players agree that the game isn't leading anywhere conclusive.

Indeed. Chess avoids this by forcing a draw if no piece has been
captured in 50 moves (IIRC). It is still a draw, though. Draws can be
avoided by forbidding repetition of positions and declaring a player the
loser if he has no legal move under this restriction. This is more or
less what Go does.

You play two games of, say, Hex one after the other, switching who
starts. If one player wins both games, he wins, otherwise it is a draw.

This is perfectly playable (and quite often how it is played in
practise) and there is no move-order advantage, as any move-order
advantage in Hex is eliminated by playing both first and second in the
two games. A player can in the second game try to mirror the first
game, but at best he can force a draw this way, so this is not a
first-player or second-player advantage.
