The initial placement of the queen by white, while strong, is not
decisive. There are two main lines of defense: 1. black can also
bring out attacking pieces so that the white king forces the black king
to be placed and a battle is fought, or 2. black places garbage to
block, e.g. pawns, then if white starts to capture pawns, black places
peices in quasi-checking/checkmating positions, white must now retreat and
defend. remember: any early capture not only gives up a tempo, but
if the defense is properly placed, allows the defense to place with
both check and a threat to a piece.

As to the difference between Q>c6 vs. Q>c3, I favor the former, since
I like my king *behind* the opposing pawns, so I never need to worry
about a placed pawn check. It seems that it is a matter of taste:
security behind your own pawns (if they ever get there), or attack
potential and security from pawn attack.
