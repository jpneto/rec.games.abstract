theodore hwa (hwatheod@leland.Stanford.EDU) wrote:
: There's a game like this called Attaxx which involves both moving and
: placing pieces. On a 7x7 board, each player starts out with 2 pieces in
: opposite corners. On any turn, you can either place a piece adjacent to
: one of yours, or move a piece 2 spaces horizontally, vertically,
: diagonally, or knight's move. In either case, any opponent's pieces
: adjacent to the newly occupied square (the piece just placed or the
: destination square of the move) are flipped. You must move if you can; if
: not, you pass. Game ends when all 49 squares are filled, and is won by
: the player with more pieces.

Ya - I've played that one, and written a program for it. The trouble
with it is that long term goals (have the most pieces) can be achieved
by the same short term goal - that is, writing a simple piece maximizer
and burying it in a 4-ply search is a very good Attaxx player, far better
than me.

One of the nice things about Othello is that maximizing the piece count
is a terrible short-term strategy, even though it is the long term
goal, which IMHO adds depth to the game that isn't there in attaxx.

I'll have to check out the moving-piece Othello game sometime soon.
