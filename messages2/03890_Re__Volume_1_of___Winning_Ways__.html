I am sympathetic to the need to obtain the books one needs, but this
particular crusade seems to be a case of the classical fallacy of
special pleading. Assuming you get Acadmic Press to keep the book
permanently in print, or even if you manage to get a copy for
your own needs, your interest in the general question of keeping
books in print (or otherwise available) is likely to evaporate.

Consider for example: Legendre's Traite des Fonctions Elliptiques
has apparently not been reprinted in its entirety for over 150 years.
Where are the publicly available editions of Gauss' collected works?
The collected works of Felix Klein were reprinted by Johnson
reprint decades ago but are now out of print. Ditto for the Klein-Fricke
treatise. Dover used to publish in 3 volumes the experimental researches
of Faraday but apparently no longer.

While on the subject, as far as I know, the unabridged edition
of My Secret Life is also long out of print and, moreover, the
abridged edition has no index. Who will volunteer to write the
indignant letter to Grove Press?

I'm glad someone is taking an interest in this general problem.
