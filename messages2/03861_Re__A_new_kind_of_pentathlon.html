Geenius at Wrok <geenius@albany.net> writes:
> On Tue, 31 Mar 1998, Joao Pedro Neto wrote:
> > * One event of mixed mental/social type,
> >
> > Herein, I decide to propose the game "diplomacy", since
> > it's a great and popular game that mixes strategic planning
> > with a complex social interaction.
>
> Diplomacy is fun, but I'm not sure it's fair in an event like your
> pentathlon. Individual sports generally boil down to one of two forms:
> pushing the limits of quantifiable performance or prevailing over another
> individual. In a footrace, even though you're racing, say, seven other
> people at once, you're gauged on a single measure -- time -- and that
> would be the same whether you were running with 20 opponents or none. In
> wrestling, the results aren't quantifiable, but you only have one opponent
> at a time whom you need to beat. In Diplomacy, you have to prevail over
> SIX other people at once in a nonquantifiable way. There are too many
> variables to make it a viable individual sport.

Well, winner-takes all diplomacy provides a port sort of a large field
of competitors. However, I believe that diplomacy *is* individually
quantifiable. Each player in the game gets 1 point for each supply
center controlled at the end of the game, is one simple rule. Another
might be 1 point per supply center per turn of control. Both of these
rules provide a total ordering of the players, without dramatically
altering the game psychology (the core component of Diplomacy).

> Moreover, on a purely abstract level, if you're competing on the basis of
> individual prowess, why does there need to be a "social" event? That's
> the province of team sports.

Its a social event in the sense that it tests your ability to deal
with complex social situations involving differing motivations and
philosophies. As far as I know, this is the *only* widely known game
which does this.

Nomic might also address these skills, but I've yet to play it and it
doesn't seem to be as well-known as Diplomacy.
