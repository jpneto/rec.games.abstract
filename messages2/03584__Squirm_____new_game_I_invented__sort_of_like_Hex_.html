NAME: "Squirm"


BRIEF DESCRIPTION: Squirm is a multiplayer abstract strategy game
with simple rules but complex strategies.


VERSION: 1.0 beta


INVENTOR: Zooko, with help from John Tromp and Francine


THE BOARD: A field of hexes forming a hexagonal board. Three of
the sides of the board are 3 hexes long, and the other three sides
are 5 hexes long.

Bigger boards can be used for longer, more complicated games or for
more players.


NUMBER OF PLAYERS: At least two.


RULES:

1. Take turns.
2. On each turn, you may place one stone of your color into an
unoccupied hex, or else you may pass.
3. The game ends when all players pass.


SNAKES:

A "snake" is a string of stones of the same color, forming a curvy
shape. Snakes have to have the following properties:

1. Each stone in the snake is adjacent to either one or two other
stones of the same color. No stone in the snake can be adjacent to
three or more stones of the same color. That is: a snake has no
branches in its body.

2. No stone in the snake is adjacent to a stone of the same color,
unless the other stone is also part of the snake. That is: a
snake cannot touch other stones of the same color unless they are
part of the snake.

3. There are no "straight lines" (that is: sequences of three
adjacent stones all in a line) in a snake.


SCORING:

At the end of the game, the person with the longest snake wins.

ALTERNATE RULES:

* The John Tromp alternate scoring rule: each snake is worth 2^x,
where x is its length.

* The "no weenies" alternate scoring rule: each snake is worth
x-n, where x is its length and n is a constant.

* The "long straight snakes" alternate snake definition: ignore
item 3 in the traditional definition of a snake. This would
completely change game play. As far as I know nobody has tried it
yet. If you do, let me know how it plays!

* The "loops bonus" rule: if you place a stone which forms a snake
in which all stones are adjacent to exactly two other snake-stones
(i.e. a loop), you get to make another move. You might choose to
allow snake eggs as loops for this rule!

* New game: a descendant of Squirm which allows you to move
stones...



RUMINATIONS:

Getting the first turn isn't necessarily an advantage, because when
you play you are committing to positions that your opponent can use
against you. Your own stones become poisonous to you in certain
situations, so it might well be that playing second is more
advantageous. This will be determined by experience, I guess.
