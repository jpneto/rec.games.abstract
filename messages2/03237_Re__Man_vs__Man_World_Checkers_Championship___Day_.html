Don't judge him too harshly unless you have inside knowledge about him
and the checkers culture. Personally, I know nothing of the checkers
culture, but in chess or Go, my sense is that this match-strategy
would not be judged unethical by other high-ranking players.

Physical health and mental intensity affect one-another both
directions. Intense concentration burns lots of calories and causes
lots of stress. Physical and mental fitness cannot be separated so
easily, so once you begin placing expectations on each player to
respond to the other's health issues, you're opening quite a can of
worms.

If the checkers community thought his behavior was unethical, then
perhaps the rules to the match should be changed.
