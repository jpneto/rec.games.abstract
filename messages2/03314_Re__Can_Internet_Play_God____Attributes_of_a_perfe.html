> The answer is directly in front of your eyes and yet you cannot see
>it. Games are mentally stimulating AND emotionally stimulating. A good
>game (I don't believe in perfection as an absolute) may teach us to
>recognize patterns, throw a ball, or read expressions, but it differs
>from a text on how to do the same thing in important ways.
> To start off, it teaches in an intuitive manner, providing us with
>an unconscious heuristic (if we get it) to add to our mental 'toolbox'.
>This allows us to more easily make connections between what we learned
>and what we already know or will later experience. Also, more relevant
>to the importance of combining the two values, the fact that it
>entertains us while it teaches us means that we are more likely to take
>up the challenge in the first place, stick with it when the going gets
>tough, and keep our attention focussed on the matters at hand.
> Approaching from the other direction, a game teaches us at the same
>time that it entertains. Different types of activities provide us with
>entertainment and emotional support in different ways. In general,
>games tend to provide entertainment of a better sort than recreational
>sex or drugs (or at least a safer, more structured environment). Too,
>the type of game that will entertain an individual tends to reflect his
>or her aesthetic inclinations. Thus they provide a good initial 'test'
>when we are searching for kindred spirits, reliable partners, or
>entertainingly stupid companions. During the game itself we form social
>bonds and learn/make our place in the group. Besides, what's so trivial
>about being entertained?

>MAX Da Wolfe

Great reply, please let me add something.
I don't believe there IS such a thing as a perfect game. I believe
that games fall under the realm of art. And as in other arts, certain games
are timeless, whereas others are fads.
So saying, here are some qualities I like to see in games:

1) Rules relatively easy to learn and definitely straightforward.
2) Replayability
3) May be played and enjoyed at different levels.
4) Keeps your interest even when it's not your turn.
