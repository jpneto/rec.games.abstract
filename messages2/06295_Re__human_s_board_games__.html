> A quick history lesson on the game of Hex:
> ------------------------------------------ In 1949, John Nash proved
> that in the game of Hex the first player has a theoretical win, but his
> proof is non-constructive; i.e., it does not indicate HOW to win.

> The proof goes like this: Either there is a winning strategy for the
> first player, or there is a winning strategy for the second player.

Hold it right there. If everything depends on this statement you can't
possibly prove a winning strategy. Only by RAA can you make anything of the
above starting point and that will show that there is no winning strategy.

> Suppose there were a winning strategy for the second player. The first
> player could then "steal" this strategy by making an arbitrary first
> move and then pretending to be the second player from then on. Whenever
> the strategy would call for playing on a hexagon he already occupied
> (e.g. because that was his arbitrary first play), the first player would
> just make another arbitrary move. His extra stone could never hurt him.
> Thus, the first player would win, which is a contradiction, therefore
> the second player does not have a winning strategy, therefore the first
> player does.

All of which is so much hogwash if there is no winning strategy for either
player.
