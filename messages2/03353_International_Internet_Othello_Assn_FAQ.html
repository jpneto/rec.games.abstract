1) What is IIOA?
----------
The International Internet Othello Association was founded in June 1996.

The following statement of purpose has been adopted by IIOA's governing
council:

------
STATEMENT OF PURPOSE

The purpose of the International Internet Othello Association is
to promote the game of Othello on the Internet.
------

The practical applications of this are still being developed, but may
include coordination and standardization of Internet-based resources,
tournaments and servers; mailing lists and newsgroups related to Othello;
and promotion of Internet-based Othello play.

Also planned is an Internet-based Othello Hall of Fame which will
recognize outstanding Othello players, Internet and otherwise.


2) Who can join IIOA?
----------
Anyone interested in Othello -- although, for practical purposes, it's
necessary that you have some form of Internet access, at the least an
e-mail address.

You may join IIOA through the membership form on the World Wide Web,
http://www.ummed.edu:8000/pub/k/khall/NewMember.html or by sending e-mail
to Kevin.Hall@ummed.edu in the following format:

{name}
{e-mail}
{postal address}
{country}
+{country code} {region/city/area code} {number}
(If you live in the U.S., use 1 as your country code:
+1 615 5552044)

There's an internet mailing list for delivering the latest information to
IIOA members; as a member, you'll be automatically subscribed.

Membership continues to apply until you miss voting in two consecutive
IIOA general elections. So far, there's no budget and thus no need for
dues.


3) How and where is Othello played on the Internet?
----------
The most common on-line locations for playing Othello:

Internet Othello Server (IOS) -- telnet to faust.uni-paderborn.de 5000
Japan Internet Othello Server (JIOS) -- telnet to 165.93.117.31 5000
Internet Gaming Zone (IGZ) -- http://www.zone.com (This site, recently
acquired by Microsoft, offers many different games, including the
generic game reversi, the rules of which are nearly identical to
Othello.)

None of these sites is operated by IIOA, but one of the projects IIOA
hopes to undertake is a system for developing one universal ratings list
which would take into account games played in a variety of Internet
settings.

The two telnet-based servers display the game board in text mode, with Xs
and Os as the pieces. Each time the position changes, an entire new
board must be scrolled onto the screen, sometimes pushing aside messages
from other players.

But a variety of ``client'' programs can be used to convert the IOS or
JIOS display into the standard green board with black and white pieces.
These clients also enable you to use ``trust'' mode, meaning that your
moves are being timed by your own computer rather than the server. That
keeps you from being unfairly penalized when the Internet is running
slowly.

Log onto IOS and type ``help clients'' for more information.


4) What about play by e-mail?
----------
Of course, any two players can play through e-mail by agreeing to swap
moves using standard Othello notation. But there's a special
``Play-by-e-mail'' server for many games, including Othello. The server
keeps track of board position, maintains a ratings list, keeps an archive
of old games and performs other useful functions. This is a boon to those
whose Internet access is limited to e-mail, as well as those who like the
pace of e-mail play.

To find out more, send e-mail to pbmserv@eiss.erols.com with the subject
line ``help.''

Play by e-mail is another area IIOA hopes to promote. Colin Springer
(springer@math.umn.edu) will be supervising IIOA's work with regards to
e-mail tournaments.


5) Are there tournaments and leagues for Othello play on the Internet?
----------
There certainly are! Here are just a few examples:

On IOS, there are tournaments on the first Saturday of each month. The
start time is rotated from month to month in fairness to players from
different time zones. Some feature both humans and computer programs,
others are humans-only.

There are also ongoing ``ladder'' tournaments. In a ladder tournament,
each player is matched during a given round with either the player ranked
directly above or below on the ladder. In case of an upset, the two
players involved switch places on the ladder. Different tournaments are
available depending on how often you want to play and how much time is
allotted to each player to make all of his/her moves in a particular
game.

The newest addition is the World Othello League, in which players
complete a regular schedule of games against divisional and
non-divisional opponents.

For more information on specific events and competitions, check out
bulletins on IOS, JIOS, IGZ or the various Othello mailing lists.


6) Are there newsgroups and Internet mailing lists?
----------
There's not currently a Usenet group related to Othello, although that
is discussed from time to time. Currently, rec.games.abstract is the only
place on Usenet for Othello discussion.

There are several mailing lists related to Othello:

othello-discuss
othello-announce
othello-program
othello-ladder

These are all English language lists. Othello-announce is moderated, the
others are not. You can subscribe to any of them at
http://www.csd.uu.se/~hugo/othello/mail/index.html or by sending e-mail
to (name of list)-request@minsk.docs.uu.se -- for example,
othello-discuss-request@minsk.docs.uu.se. The subject line should read
``subscribe'' if you want to subscribe, or ``update'' if you're just
updating your information. The body of the message should look like
this:

EMAIL: {your e-mail address}
NAME: {your real name}
IOS: {your IOS login name, if any}
COMMENT:

To unsubscribe, follow the same procedure, but with ``unsubscribe'' as
your subject line and only the ``EMAIL: {your e-mail address}'' line in
the message.

For information about mailing lists in languages other than English,
contact:

Japanese: Tetsuya Nakajima, jios@itolab.ei.tuat.ac.jp
Italian: Paolo Fasce, fasce@mbox.cis.it
Nordic, Sweden, Uppsala: Hugo Calendar, hugo@minsk.docs.uu.se

Also, there are the specific mailing lists related to iioa and its
governing council: iioa-members@update.uu.se and
iioa-council@update.uu.se. These lists are for the use of the stated
groups, and you'll be automatically subscribed if you qualify.

9) What's the Othello Hall of Fame?
----------
The Othello Hall of Fame will be a web site honoring the world's greatest
Othello players (it is not limited to Internet play). Initially, the
hall will include all the players who have participated in World
Championship finals; after the hall has been established, IIOA will
review the criteria and set up a mechanism for identifying and adding
other worthy players. George Ortiz (froggy@blitzen.canberra.edu.au) is
now collecting information and scannable photos of the honorees; please
contact him if you've got something to contribute!


10) Does IIOA have an official language?
----------
IIOA meetings and business are conducted in English, but given our
international nature we plan to make every reasonable effort to collect
information from and distribute it to non-English speakers. Kevin Hall
(Kevin.Hall@ummed.edu) is IIOA's translation director.
