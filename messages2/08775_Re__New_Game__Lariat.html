Well, enough people have had trouble with the origami cylinder that I
don't want to derail readers before they even get going. I think it's
best to present the lariat principle and get that established, and
then bump it onto a cylinder.

>
> Anyway, it's obviously not at all certain yet, what the appropriate
> ratio between cap-size and cylinder length should be, to make it
> a fair game, and would need a lot of playing to determine this.

Yes that is certainly true. But I think some observations can be made
at this point. There should be a range. Like the ratio of (radius +
length)/circumference should be *at least* x to make the "key" cell
strategically undesirable as a first or second move. More
specifically, (radius + length) should be larger than the
circumference. That's the main thing. Then the cylinder shouldn't be
excessively long just because the outer reaches probably won't be used
and you end up wasting screen real estate.
