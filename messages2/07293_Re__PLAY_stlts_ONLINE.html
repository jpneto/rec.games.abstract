I realise I'm playing the computer and may well be a bit smarter than
the available AI (I hope!), but it strikes me that there's too much to
be gained by just sticking lots of pins into a piece and then mopping up
stuff at will. A second piece with more limited mobility can be used as
a "stooge" to sacrifice to break up a mutually protective pair, and for
pieces outside of clusters a piece that can go, pretty much, anywhere on
the board in a single turn is maybe too powerful.

In games like DVONN the pieces that can travel further actually lose
maneuverability as their range increases... I wonder if something like
that might be used to limit the power of uber-pieces, or make developing
a range of pieces more strategically useful than just building up one
for the first few moves followed by hoovering?

If folk playing Real People have found easy counters to this then this
can be disregarded, but as it is it seems a pretty foolproof way to
squish the robot player.
