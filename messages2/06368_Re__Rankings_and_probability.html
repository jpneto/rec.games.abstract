Exactly - and with more modern sports, the difference in skill levels
between opponents at the professional level is usually not huge. I
actually modified the program to give each player a "skill level", and
varied them so that teams were classified such that on average, they
were grade from half as likely up to twice as likely to win a game
than the average skill level. The final rankings showed only a vague
correspondence to programmed-in skill level. The only way to combat
this is of course to play far far more games, which in real life means
that the only really meaningful stats about a player or team's skill
level are long term stats - i.e. those collected over several years.
Of course the problem in many sports is that the player's and team's
skill levels due change quite significantly from year to year due to
injury, illness, retirements, drafting (in team sports) etc. etc.
