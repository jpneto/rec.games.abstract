> So, abstract strategy games don't involve pieces and boards? Come on
> Mark, you can't define an abstract strategy game without defining it
> as a boardgame. Why do you want to remove this? It is fine to expand

The point is not that they all have them, but that all other board
games have them too. We don't define humans as "mammals with
hearts", even though humans are mammals with hearts. Since all
mammals have hearts the information is tautological, so it doesn't
/help/ us.

It needs work to show how an ASG board and pices are different from
a non-ASG board and pieces, and starting off the document with it
as "key elements" is like starting a text on cheese with how to
milk cows: while ultimately a crucial part of the process you don't
actually hve to know how to do it to make cheese, and if you've
already got a bucket of milk it just gets in the way.
