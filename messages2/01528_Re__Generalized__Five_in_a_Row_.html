>(Not sure if this isn't too trivial for your forum...)

>I was thinking about modifications of the simple `Five-in-a-row' game
>(two players on a go board, winner is who first makes a contiguous line
>of five stones of his colour). (It is called `gobang' sometimes.)
>
>Generalize this to (m,n)-gobang: m players, aiming at n stones in a row.
>So (2,5) is standard, (2,4) is trivial (first player always wins).
>You might enjoy (3,4) which is not at all trivial. Funny situations
>arise where you're forced to cooperate against your will or where
>you let the third player respond to a threat against both him and yourself.
>
>My wild guess is that on an infinite board, for m>1, there's an n
>such that a strategy exists (for one player) which guarantees that none
>of the m players ever can make a n-row. Is this true for m=2 at least?
>
>Johannes - mwj@rz.uni-jena.de


Yes, there is such a strategy for n=7. (two players) It involves tiling
the board with a pattern (with a size near 7x7) that tells you where to go
if your opponent makes any move inside that particular pattern.
Something like that. I think the reference is Winning Ways Vol. 2 by
Berlecamp Canway and Guy.
