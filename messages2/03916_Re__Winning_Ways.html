Can mathematics be copyrigthed? I am willing to take that risk. Basically,
both players take turns and the last one with a legal move wins. So, let's
take a nim variant where there are two heaps, every player has his own heap
and a turn is to take one from one's own heap. This game is not too
difficult to analyze (in fact, it could be used to learn a two-year old
some concepts on numbers, but it would probably be not interesting enough
for that, unless it is played with cookies):

Let p be the number of items on my heap minus the number of items on your heap:

p > 0 : my heap is larger than yours; I win, no matter who starts the game.
p < 0 : my heap is smaller than yours; you win, no matter who starts the game
p = 0 : both heaps are equally large, he who starts the game looses

p || 0 does not occur with this game. If I remember correctly this is
called 'p is confused with 0'. It happens in a simple NIM-game with one
heap, with one item left.

The fun thing is that one can add games (where each player must play in
exactly one game in the sum of games): The first game with p = 0 plus the
second game gives you a game where the beginner wins, e.g. a game with p ||
0. He ends the second game by taking the bean, and forces his opponent to
start playing the p == 0 game.

Volume 1 of Winning ways is filled with determining how to add up these
'nimbers' (I haven't read 'Surreal numbers', but assume that these are the
same numbers), and classifying games according to their 'nim value'. This
leads to categorizations such as 'hot games', where making a move is a good
thing to do. The first game above, for instance, is certainly not hot. If
my heap has N items, I have N moves there, and there is nothing I nor my
opponent can do to change that.
