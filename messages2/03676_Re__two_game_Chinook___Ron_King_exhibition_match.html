This was the situation. Ron King was playing in a quiet room.
The Internet connection to that room was broken, hence Chinook
had to be operated in another room. I operated Chinook, and
then had to use a walkie-talkie to relay the moves to the room
that King was playing in. I had a graduate student receive
the moves, play them on the board, push the clock and relay
moves back to me. The student was unfamiliar with checkers
and checkers notation, as well as playing tournament games.

Since I was not in the playing room, this is all that I know.
Chinook's flag fell on or about move 30. At the time, King
did not claim a win on time. The game continued and King
resigned one or two moves later. After resigning, he then
claimed a win on time. As someone familiar with chess rules,
I beleive that King had no basis for a claim:
(1) he did not keep a scoresheet
(2) he played the game out to the end and resigned before
making his claim.
When Chinook's flag fell, he should have stopped the game and
claimed a win. He did not do so, so it was impossible for me
(and the arbiter) to verify King's claim.

Since this was an exhibition match with nothing at stake, we
agreed to a draw. The final position is, of course, an easy
win for Chinook.
