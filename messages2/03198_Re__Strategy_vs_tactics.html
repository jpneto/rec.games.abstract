Humans have been playing chess for about 1500 years (less for chess in
its current form). Considering that computers have been playing chess
for about 30 years, it shows the state of the art in programming and
computer design has progressed at a VERY rapid pace.

(There are other factors that make chess more amenable to design of
computer opponent than many other games: No random factors, well
established opening books, reasonable analysis algorithms for analysis
etc.)

: My questions are these: what (deterministic) games exist in which there

: What would such a game involve? Lots of pieces? Lots of functionality? Is
: the idea to make the tactics so hopelessy complex that strategy would be
: more effective, or is it possible somehow to have strategy without
: tactics?
: I'd be interested to see folk's views on the relative strategy / tactics
: ratio of the common games (chess, go, othello, draughts etc.)


I think most people would say Go and Shogi are much more difficult for
several reasons:
- Large number of possible moves per turn. (Go simply has a lot of
space on the board, Shogi with its "drop" also has many more possible
moves per turn) (In mid game of a typical game: a chessplayer has
30-40 possible moves, a Go player or Shogi player 200-300 (or more!)
possible moves.
- Go has very intricate and hard to describe (for computer programming
purposes) tactics and strategy. In Chess, 1 pawn in a slighlty
different location usually doesn't completely change of the analysis of
a position, where as in Go a change of one piece out of 100 on the board
does change the analysis frequently.)

Other games seem to be easier do design good computer opponents seem to
have limited numbers of moves per turn and/or easier to describe
strategies:
- Checkers (draughts) has relatively few options per turn and often has
forced captures. Also since the goal is annihilation of the opponent,
analysis generally falls on finding moves which result in unequal
exchanges (2 for 1, etc.)
- Othello also has relatively few options per turn (An othello player
might have 1-30 possible moves (but usually 10-20 range).)

In the case of other games, development might be behind chess because
either A) there is a smaller market for computer opponents for these
games. or B) There is less glamour to produce computer AI (If a Go
playing computer was developed that was possibly good enough to beat the
best masters in the world, would there be as much interest as there is
in the current Kasparov-Deep Blue match? Except in Japan, i think
probably not.)
