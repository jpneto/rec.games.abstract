should we add some obvious things:

any good game should have a clear answer to the "mirror"
strategy. games should not be susceptable to tyro strategy.

i also like the concept of "Beginning, Middle, End". i also
like the concept of the honorable "Resign" over dragging out
a lost position. this may be at odds with "The Game Should End
Soon", in that the very premise that it is better to Resign in
some cases, posits the notion that the ending may be "not soon
enough". there should still be an honorable way to Resign, yet
no part of the game should be unimportant.... a tough nut. ok-
not obvious. we have discussed alot of obvious.

another note i saw:

the game should be rich in opening moves, no 2 games should
resemble each other for very long.

thoughts? i will adhere to this principle only as far as i can
see that deja' vu isnt what we're looking for here. but isnt this
a direct refusal of the concept of memorization of openings. i find
myself falling into a crowd of people who despise opening memorization.
i hate chess! even go has this horrid characteristic! computers
have (still) perfect memory, and i do not want to aspire to becoming
a computer. for example, i thought of a go game as follows: have a
random generator put 60 stones of each color on the board and give the
resultant position to the 2 contestants. each makes a very educated &
careful estimate of the who is ahead by how much. if they both give the
same estimates, recast the position and repeat. if one player says Black
by 6 and the other says Black by 25, give the first White and the other
Black and the komi is (6+25)/2 = 15.5.
