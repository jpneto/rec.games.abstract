Multiplayer LaserTank game idea.

From: caravel@halcyon.com (Erik Hermansen)
Date: 22 11 1997 00:00:00 GMT


I've had some fun playing Jim Kindley's LaserTank game. If you want
to see what I'm talking about then download it from this URL:

http://www.crl.com/~jcc/jek/tank.html

Consider some drastic changes made to the game:

1. Multiplayer, with 10 to 100 people playing at once.
2. Each player controls one lasertank. His score increases whenever
he successfully destroys other tanks.
3. If a player gets killed or self-destructs (necessary if the player
is trapped) then his score decreases.
4. A tank can destroy another tank by firing at it.
5. Tanks move over a gigantic scrolling grid (say 1000x1000) with game
elements scattered randomly over it and certain geographic features to
be strategically exploited. A player can only see the area his tank
is in, so there will always be things going on that he doesn't know
about.
6. For simplicity, just assume all of the game elements that are
placed on the grid act like the ones in the existing LaserTank game.
7. Laser fire from tanks and guns have a range that is limited so that
you can't be hit by offscreen tanks and guns.
8. Players enter and leave the game whenever they want.
9. Players can form alliances with other players. An alliance makes
allied tanks invulnerable to each other's fire and their score goes
up/down if allies kill or get killed.
10. When a player leaves the game, his tank remains on the game grid
and is vulnerable to attacks from other players. He resumes control
of the tank when he reenters the game.

Because of this last point, a lot of time would be spent by players
building fortresses to "sleep" inside of while they were offline. You
build a fortress by pushing blocks, guns, and deflectors around so
that they will make it difficult for an enemy to enter a space.

Directly confronting active tanks in an open shootout is not a winning
strategy, because whoever sits still to defend himself will have the
advantage. If you think about how the tanks are controlled in
LaserTank, this becomes clearer. The key is that a tank must turn
before it can move in a direction, and can only fire in a direction it
is turned. A tank must be in the same row or column with another and
turned correctly to shoot it. If one tank is smart, he will stand
still and wait for the other tank to enter the same row or column as
him. As soon as the moving tank enters the in-line row or column, the
stationary tank will kill him, because stationary tank will aready be
turned to face the exposed moving tank that will be turned to its side
as it enters. Aggressive tactics are likely to get a tank killed out
in the open, so he is better to form strategies that use the blocks,
mirrors, and guns.

An interesting property of the pushable blocks, guns, and mirrors is
that it seems impossible to create a fortress that is both
invulnerable to invasion from outside and allows tanks from inside to
leave. "Seems"--I could be wrong.

You could build a fortress with pushable blocks and have the wall be 2
blocks wide all the way around. Then there would be no way to get in
or out. You'd be safe, but trapped.

I experimented with other types of fortresses but always the invader
could theoretically get in or trap you by pushing blocks, guns, and
mirrors around outside the fortress. It seems like the type of
fortress best able to withstand an attack would be a complex and
dynamic one. Whoever was inside the fortress would have to keep
changing it--adding traps; moving guns to different entrances in
response to outside movements; calling for help to allies outside the
fort; disrupting objects that the enemy has placed outside the fort;
looking for a way to shoot the enemy when he is not expecting it; and
all sorts of other activities.

If nobody was attacking your fort at the moment, it might be a good
idea to go off and attack someone else's. You want to defend your
fort though because it gets bigger and better as you put more effort
into it, and you don't want your work to fall to rubble or get taken
over by your enemies.

And since you can't play all the time, you'd need to have allies to
defend the fort when you were offline. Success in the game would
depend on planning, building, and allying. A lone gunner would have a
tough time staying alive longer than one session, because you need a
safe place to stay while you are offline.

I'd be interested to see if anyone can come up with a LaserTank
fortress that is constructed only with guns, pushable blocks, and
mirrors; invulnerable to an attack (you have to imagine a second tank
shooting and pushing things around to either get inside or block off
the entrances so the defender is trapped); and allows the constructor
a way in an out of it. I don't think it is possible, and that is what
would make this type of game appealing--any type of arrangement can be
broken down with enough effort.

What do you think?
