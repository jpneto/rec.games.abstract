The Peace SIG of the Cleveland FreeNet system announces the first
world-wide session of "Global Futures", a on-line version of a world
economic and political simulation game. The original game is copyright
1975 by Earthrise and is played by teams of players in the same room with
paper scoresheets. Our version will feature teams of players
communicating with each other on-line, via email and (if your network
access permits) bulletin board messages and real-time electronic chats.
The resulting scoresheets for each round will be published in an
electronic newsletter, a more frequent version of which will also be
available as a public forum for those players with only e-mail access.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS From the manual: "The GLOBAL FUTURES GAME is a dynamic
simulation of present and future world conditions in
terms of population, food, technology and education and the relative
growth rates of each. Using real-world statistics as futurists and
policy-makers, groups of players representing eight socio-economic regions
barter for resourcees in five-year rounds."

Briefly, each team starts with a given number of units in three areas:
population, food, and technology, and with a scoresheet into which is
built the rate of increase of each kind of unit for that region. Each
region may also use food or technology units to invest in education, which
will pay off starting two rounds hence by helping to curb population and
increase food and technology. The idea is to keep the ratios of these
units to a reasonable level, which is sometimes not that easy! During the
course of each of the nine rounds, each team negotiates trades of their
available resources with the other regions, and/or shuffles its units
around internally as the rules allow. At the end of the each round the
results are calculated and the new round begins. An individual region
"wins" when it reaches zero population growth and the desired ratio of
units (food and technology both at twice the population) by the end of
nine rounds. If 10 "World Destruct Points" are achived (through certain
non-desirable ratios), everyone loses, no matter which region caused the
points.

HOW WE WILL PLAY IT In the normal game, the players are all in the same
room, free to negotiate face-to-face, and each round
lasts 15 minutes. Needless to say, our version will have to be a little
bit different.

>>> Communication: It is assumed that all players have regular access to
some computer account allowing mail to be sent and received to and from
the InterNet, the network on which the Cleveland FreeNet resides. For
players for whom this is the only option, an email newsletter will be sent
out semi-regularly (as often as each day or as infrequently as every
several days, as volume warrants and my schedule permits) listing the
email address of all players and as a public forum for email announcements
to the entire "world". (You may also send email to any configuration of
players you wish, since you have all their addresses; the newsletter is
only to faciliate sending messages to everyone so you don't have to go to
the trouble of making a distribution list and so on.) You may then use
private email to communicate with each region or send any public
announcements you have to me for inclusion.

Any other network communication methods you may have available are also
allowable, as you may be able to work out with the other players, such as
on-line chats and so on. Since I don't know from here what is available
to you on your network, I cannot help you on this.

Finally, if your system permits remote logins (using "telnet" or some
other program), you may wish to consider getting a FreeNet account.
Details of this are below. On FreeNet, we will maintain a public board
which may be used to post messages to other regions, and to which each
round's results will be posted. For email-only players, all messages
posted here will be reproduced in the newsletter for your perusal, and any
messages sent to me indicating they are to be posted will be posted to
this board.

>>> Rounds: Each round in the network game will last 2 weeks, Monday
morning to the Sunday evening two weeks hence. At the close of each round
(probably the Monday evening following each change of round), results will
be tabulated, checked for consistency, and posted to the board and the
newsletter. One player for each region should be designated as liason to
the moderator and will be responsible for emailing me all a file listing
all transactions for that round.

>>> Regions and Players: The game involves 8 regions corresponding roughly
to continents, with up to 10 players per region. Players should feel free
to split the responsibility for that region however they see fit. For
example, you may wish to designate one person to be in charge of each type
of unit, another person as ambassador to other regions (or several for
different regions), one in charge of keeping tabs on all transactions and
mailing them in, and so on. The basic actions of the game are simple, but
there are certain subtleties which may make it worth specializing within a
region.

Please note that for fairness to other players, only players who actually
have email accounts are counted as official players. If you want to get
several of your non-networked friends involved, or your classroom, that's
fine, but if we get a lot of people we may still have to count only
players with InterNet addresses and assign other people to play your
region with you to round out the teams.

HOW TO GET INVOLVED Simply send email to "aa713@cleveland.freenet.edu"
(w/o the quotes) and request to be a player.
Please note that participation in a previous game does not automatically
sign you up for this one. When you sign up, you will be sent an
information packet containing the complete rules, example scoresheets,
details of the regions, and how to sign up for them. Please do not
request a region in your initial message, as that region may already
filled up. The information packet will let you know how many players have
already signed up for each region, and at that time you can email me about
which one you want to play. All regions are first-come, first-serve by
players who have received their information packet.

Please indicate at some point before the game starts if you will be
playing by email only or if you have a FreeNet account and some reliable
way of getting to it. The email newsletter will be sent out only to those
with email-only access, so that those reading the board will not be
bothered with duplicate information.

If you do not already have a FreeNet account and wish to look into getting
one, here is some information about that. FreeNet is, as the name
implies, free of charge. Unfortunately, there is not an 800 number, so
either you should have some sort of remote login capability (ask your
system operator, I have no way of knowing whether you do or not) or be
willing to call long-distance to Cleveland, Ohio. Another unfortunate
problem with the public phone lines are that they are almost always busy.
The phone number, if you still want to try, is (216) 368-3888. If you can
do remote access from your local system, you want to connect to
"freenet-in-a.cwru.edu" (or freenet-in-b... or freenet-in-c...). You may
log in as a guest if you want to look it over before you sign up, which
you can do on-line. The board on which this activity will take place is
the "Global Futures Game" board of the FreeNet Peace SIG. From any
menu-level prompt, enter "go peace" to get the Peace SIG, then take option
9 for the game board.

If you decide you want an account, keep in mind you'll probably want it
ready to go by the time the game starts. If you sign up on-line, they
will send you a verification form by postal mail which you need to fill
out and return before they will activate your account. Once they get the
verification they are usually pretty speedy about turning it on.
