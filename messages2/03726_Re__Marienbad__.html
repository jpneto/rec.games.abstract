>Years ago I learned a game called Marienbad which involves sixteen
>matchsticks arranged in rows of 1-3-5-7.
Also called Nim.

>Players alternate taking
>sticks from any single row, any number of sticks. The object is to
>_avoid_ having to pick up the last matchstick.
>I was once told there is some bizarre mathematics involved in deciding
>which is the next correct move. Can anybody enlighten me on this?
Described in one of Martin Gardner's books. In fact, the win is exactly
the same as if the last player to take the match wins

1. Convert each row to binary, like so:

1
11
101
111

2. XOR the rows, like so:

000

3. If the total sums to zero, then the next player to move has lost.
If the total doesn't sum to zero, then the next player can win by making
the total XOR to zero. Continue until you have two rows left, and use a
little common sense, ...
