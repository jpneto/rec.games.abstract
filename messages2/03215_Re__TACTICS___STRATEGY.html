IMO tactical considerations are what guides strategical decisions:
other wise you may find yourself doing the move you would like that
works instead of the move that works. I mean, once you have got an
interesting strategic plan, (to cut and attack a group) you need to
check if a) it can be cut; b) then it can be attacked; c) you will gain
something from the attack. Usually, at least in my case, I take the
strategic decision of going for an attack after checking that it can be
done, (and that there is nothing more interesting to do).
I must say I reverse the order when after analizing the board I come
to the sad conclusion that either I rescue my group under attack or I'll
lose. Then I try to do it until time to resign comes. So it seems
there's a lot of feedback betwen tactics and strategy -what can be done
vs. what I want to do- .
