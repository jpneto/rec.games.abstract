1. Basically (an overview)
When deciding the results of an assault within a "battle", add the
separate totals of the "attack" and "Defense" capabilities for both
groups, and then divide each of these sums by six. The respective
products of these divisions represent the number of units "destroyed" in
the given single assault. (As always, the attacker retains the option to
continue the attack by launching another assault.)

Example 1:
If 6 men attack a group of 3 men: the results of the first single assault
is a loss of 1 defender, and 1 attacker.

Attacking Force: 6 men. Total attack capability = 6
Defending Force 3 men. Total defense capability = 6
------------------------------------------
Defender loss = 1 unit.
Attacker loss = 1 unit.

Example 2:
If 4 tanks attack a group comprised of 3 tanks and 6 men: the results of
this single assault are a loss of 2 defenders, and 3 attackers.

Attacking Force: 4 tanks.
Defending Force 3 tanks and 6 men.
------------------------------------------
Defender loss = 2 units.
Attacker loss = 3 units.

FRACTIONS: (the remainder of the division by 6)
The fraction, in the case of the attacker, if less than five, is ignored.
If the fraction is equal to 5, than it is considered a "hit".

In the case of the defender, the fraction is considered a hit if equal to
4 or 5.
If the fraction is less than 4, it is ignored. (The four included
variations offer alternatives to this truncation of value.)

Examples 3:
Attacking Force: 4 tanks and 5 men.
Total attack value = 17 (the fraction 5 is considered a hit)

Defending Force 3 tanks and 5 men.
Total defense value = 16 (the fraction 4 is considered a hit)
------------------------------------------
Defender loss = 3 units.
Attacker loss = 3 units.

Examples 4:
Attacking Force: 4 tanks and 4 men.
Total attack value = 16 (the fraction 4, or less, is ignored)

Defending Force 3 tanks, 4 men, and 1 bomber.
Total defense value = 15 (the fraction 3, or less, is ignored)
------------------------------------------
Defender loss = 2 units.
Attacker loss = 2 units.

2. ANTI-AIRCRAFT FIRE
Total the number of planes used within a given entire move, and divide
this total by 5; the product of this division indicates the number of
planes lost , and the fraction (4 or less) is ignored. In cases where the
planes involved in a move are disbursed among two or more territories, and
the total number of planes exceeds 4, then the commander of said aircraft
may choose which plane (or planes, in the case of more than 9 planes) will
be considered lost by anti-aircraft fire.

Examples 5:
If Germany sends 3 aircraft to the UK and 2 (up to 6) aircraft to Russia
(assuming both territories being attacked are defended with
anti-aircraft), 1 aircraft is lost before the attack totals are computed.
(Flying over multiple anti-aircraft, in a single move, increases these
totals accordingly. For example if 3 planes fly over two territories with
AA, then 1 plane is lost at the point where the total equals or exceeds
5.)

3. ECONOMIC BOMBER RUNS
In the case of economic bomber runs, the same anti-aircraft rule
modifications are used, and the attacker is awarded a 2.5 production unit
deficit (on the enemy treasury) for each bomber that survives the
"anti-aircraft fire". If the total has a fraction, it is ignored.

4. SUBMARINE FIRST SHOT
Attacking submarines are handled normally, except their attack strength is
totaled separately and divided by 6; the product of this division
indicates the number of defending units which will be removed from the
board, WITHOUT opportunity to return fire. The fraction is added to the
normal remaining attack total of the given assault.

Example 6:

Attacking Force: 2 battleship, 1 fully loaded (2 fighters) carrier, and 5
submarines.
Total attack value = 25
Defending Force: 1 battleship, 1 fully loaded (2 fighters) carrier, and 1
transport.
Total retaliatory defense value = 15
(16 - 1 : assuming the transport is selected as the casualty of the 3
submarine's first strike hit)
------------------------------------------
Defender loss = 4 units. (the remainder or fraction of "1" is ignored)
Attacker loss = 2 units. (the remainder or fraction of "3" is ignored)

5. WEAPONS DEVELOPMENT
Units having had their attack values augmented by weapons development are
treated according to their given increase. Dice rolls representing
"rocket attacks" are always considered to be a value of 3.

6. Optional Reintroduction of a Small Random Element
see 7.2, 7.3, 7.5

7. The Five Variations of A&A Chess.

7.1 Orthodox
The orthodox variation of A&A Chess is described above.

7.2 "Roll Your Own Fraction" (RYOF)
If you wish to retain a slight random element in your contest, simply roll
a single die to resolve fractions which would normally (using the orthodox
variation of A&A Chess) be ignored, i.e., remainders of 4 or less for the
attacker, and 3 or less for the defender.
(For example, if the fraction [remainder] was 3, then a die roll of 3 or
less would be considered a hit.)

7.3 "Roll Your Own Fraction less the 6" (RYOF<6)
If you desire even more uncertainty in your game, you can roll a die to
decide all fractions less than 6, including such values as AA fire,
rockets, and bomber E-run depletions on the enemy treasury.

7.4 "Carry Over the Fraction" (COF)
All fractions (< 5 for the attacker, or < 4 for the defender) of single a
"battle" (an attack on a single territory or sea zone) are "carried over"
to the respective totals of subsequent assaults within the same battle (in
the event there are multiple assaults). When the attacker withdraws, or
one side is destroyed, the final remaining relevant fraction or fractions
(if any) are ignored if < 5 for the attacker, or < 4 for the defender;
otherwise the given fraction is considered a hit.

Notice that when dealing with fractions (representing a hit: > 4 for the
attacker, or > 3 for the defender) the enemy casualties are remove from
the battle board simultaneously as they occur (during the assault in which
said fraction qualifies). Such qualifying fractions are not added again
to the next attack assault totals. In contrast, a barely qualify fraction
actually creates a deficit in the running total, which will effect any
subsequent assault; this is necessary to maintain equity when using the
"Carry Over the Fraction" method. For example, if the attacker's last
assault minimally qualified with a fraction of 5, he would have some
slight degree of incentive to withdraw, knowing he must now total another
full 6 points to gain his next hit.

Example 7: (BTW, there are programs that will do this math for you,
BATSIM and AXIS have been recommended by several players in the
newsgroup.)

Here are the details of a single battle (of four assaults), in which 4
tanks and 5 men attack 10 men. By the COF variation, the results of two
given battles of equal material configurations, will be identical in all
cases, and at each assault levels. In other words, anytime 4 tanks and 5
men attack 10 men, the results, at each assault level, will be the same as
this example 7.

(Identical forces on both sides, will reliably produce the same sequence
of assault values. Some players have expressed a concern that this would
foster standard lines of play. I believe they are correct. Chess has
standard lines of play so popular they are given names. These opening
preferences have not prevented chess from prevailing as the most revered
arena of cognitive competition in history. The analogy is flawed,
however, since A&A Chess has a much wider spectrum of play dimensions.)

1st ASSAULT:
Attacking Force: 4 tanks and 5 men.
Total attack value = 17 (divided by 6 = 2 remainder 5)
DEFENDER LOSS = 3 units. (c-1)

The 1st assault fraction 5 qualifies as a hit (in this round) for the
attacker, creating a carryover deficit of c-1 that is applied to the next
assault total of the attacker.

Defending Force: 10 men.
Total defense value = 20 (divided by 6 = 3 remainder 2)
ATTACKER LOSS = 3 units. (c2) a defense value surplus carried over to
next assault total of the defender.

The fraction 2, is 2 short of qualifying for a 4th hit by the defended in
this first assault, so only 3 unit will be removed from the attacking
force. However, a carryover value of c2 now exists for the defender, and
will be applied to the defense total in the next assault, if any.

Notice that if the attacker withdraws now, fractions (carryovers plus or
minus) are lost; such fractions are not carried to the next battle, only
to the next assault within the same battle.

2nd ASSAULT:
Attacking Force: 4 tanks 2 men
Total attack value 14 + carryover -1 = 13 (divided by 6 = 2 remainder 1)
DEFENDER LOSS = 2 units. c1

Defending Force: 7 men.
Total defense value = 14 + carryover 2 = 16 (divided by 6 = 2 with a
remainder of 4, which qualifies as a defensive hit in the current assault,
creating a -2 point defensive deficit that will be applied to the next
assault, in any)
ATTACKER LOSS = 3 units. c-2

3rd ASSAULT:
Attacking Force: 3 tanks
Total attack value 9 + c1 = 10 (divided by 6 = 1 remainder 4, not
qualifying as hit, but creating an attack surplus of c4)
DEFENDER LOSS = 1 units. c4

Defending Force: 5 men.
Total defense value = 10 + (c-2) = 8 (divided by 6 = 1 remainder 2)
ATTACKER LOSS = 1 unit. c2

4th ASSAULT:
Attacking Force: 2 tanks
Total attack value 6 + c4 = 10 (divided by 6 = 1 remainder 4)
DEFENDER LOSS = 1 units. c4 (c4 value lost)

Defending Force: 4 men.
Total defense value = 8 + c2 = 10 (divided by 6 = 1 remainder 4)
ATTACKER LOSS = 2 unit. c-2 (c-2 value lost)

Attacking units have all been eliminated. There are 3 defending units
remaining.

7.5 "Carry Over the Fraction + Roll" (COF+R)
Same as COF except a single die may be cast to resolve any remaining
relevant fraction or fractions (< 5 for the attacker, or < 4 for the
defender) in final assault of a given battle.

8. RECOMMENDATION
This last variation, A&A Chess (COF+R) offers maximum equity, lightly
seasoned with a "minimal dash of luck" on the end. However, I believe the
totally diceless (COF) variation will inevitably became the most popular.
Luck clearly plays a large role in the game of A&A, but I am convinced
that dice will eventually have "no roll" in A&A Chess.

9. E-MAIL PLAY
Any one of these modifications will provide a more meaningful contest than
the conventional "all dice" approach. Another advantage is that these new
methods virtually (orthodox and COF, completely) solve the email-play
problems of dice rolling. (The single dice roll offer very little
temptation, nor even opportunity, for indiscretions of honor). Respective
moves are simply made by each player in turn. The final configuration,
after a move, is merely sent to the opponent, along with perhaps an
explanation of anything one has done, which can not obviously be resolved.
If the receiving player cannot justify the move, then he may challenge
the move; calling for a full explanation of the issue he questions. This
has the effect of reducing the time, correspondence, and confusing
normally associated with email-play.

There are a number of rules changes being considered for diceless A&A
Chess. However, presently only the following alterations will be observed
in Official play.

1. Russia is restricted from attacking in the first round of play.

2. Anti-Aircraft Fire
Total the number of planes used within a given entire move, and divide
this total by 5; the product of this division indicates the number of
planes lost , and the fraction (4 or less) is ignored. In cases where the
planes involved in a move are disbursed among two or more territories, and
the total number of planes exceeds 4, then the commander of said aircraft
must choose which plane (or planes, in the case of more than 9 planes)
will be considered lost by anti-aircraft fire.

Examples:
If Germany sends 3 aircraft to the UK and 2 (up to 6) aircraft to Russia
(assuming both territories being attacked are defended with
anti-aircraft), 1 aircraft is lost before the attack totals are computed
on the Russian assault. (Flying over multiple anti-aircraft, in a single
move, increases these totals accordingly. For example if 3 planes fly
over two territories with AA, then 1 plane is lost at the point where the
total equals or exceeds 5.)

Additionally, in order to compensate for the loss in AA value, involving
plane exposures of less than 5 occurrences, all non-combat fly-overs are
treated the same as combat, i.e. planes may be lost in phase of a given
move as well. All occurrences of planes at risk are totaled for the
entire move. (So if one attacks a territory with 4 planes and then wishes
to fly the 4 planes over another AA enemy territory during the landing
phase, this totals 8, i.e. 1 plane will be lost in the landing phase.)

Recommendation: One popular strategy for AA, is to place a multiple number
of AA guns between threatening aircraft groups and their possible targets.

3. Weapons Development:
Weapons development will not be incorporated as an option at this time.
(This renders the corresponding section 5, of the A&A Chess Official
Rules, inapplicable for the 1995 tournament.)

Clarification On Using the COF Variation.
Strengths below 5 for the attacker (and 4 for the defender) are "carried
over" to next assault (but NOT the next battle, as with AA totals).

A few classic confrontations in COF:

When a bomber attacks a single man, both are lost (in the second assault).

A sub will defeat a single transport (in the 3rd assault) without loss.

2 men defeat a single man (in the 3rd assault), loosing 1 man (in the
second assault).

2 tanks defeat 2 men, loosing 1 tank.

3 tanks defeat 3 men, loosing 2 tanks.

3 subs defeat a battleship without loss.

11. Official Email Tournament Procedure.

Please send the IA&ACF your time zone and city, so that we can match you
up with opponents as near as possible to your zone. Also, state any
preference as to which side you would like to play, we will try to
accommodate. If case of conflict, sides will be assigned at random.

A. MOVE FORMATS - All moves must be registered with the IA&ACF, i.e.
copies sent simultaneously to: IAACF@aol.com

Enclosed is a typical example of a move. Try and stay within the format
as much as possible. If you deviate from the format, you will not
necessarily be penalized, the IA&ACF (and hopefully your opponent) will
attempt to interpret the intent of all entries. Try to be tolerant of each
others mistakes (when they are obviously of a clerical nature, ie failure
to record the location of an existing piece, etc.), and settle as many
problems as you can on your own; if you cannot, see #3. If you cannot
understand an opponents entry, you may ask for a clarification. Such a
request is considered a move; and will stop your clock, and start your
opponents clock (*). A request for a choice of casualties, or sub a
retreat option, is also considered a move.
(*) If you ask for a clarification of an entry that your opponent feels
is not unclear, he may object and request a decision from the IA&ACF (see
#3.). If the IA&ACF agrees that there was no ambiguity in the entry at
question, then the time charges will be reversed to you.

B. TIME LIMITS - Each player will have 7 days (168 hours) to make all of
his moves, regardless of how many rounds a game may go. The maximum time
allowed for a single move is 24 hours (US/Russian moves are considerd a
single move in this respect). Any player who is determined by the IA&ACF
to have exceeded either of these time limitations will automatically lose
the game by time default. Both players may agree to stop the clock, at
any point. This is called an "Agreed Pause". There is a 72 hour limit on
the combined times of all such Agreed Pauses, during a single tournamant
game.

It would be convenient for everyone, if players would attempt to keep each
other informed as to their individual time expectation. For instance, if
you intend to retire for several hours or more, after a given move, it
would be easier on both sides, if you inform your opponent. Strictly
speaking, however, such a declaration is not binding; so there are no
punitive measure in the event a given player would misinform another, as
to his intent or move actions. Try to be cooperative and considerate of
each other in all respects, and remember that people also have lives to
live during these games.

C. The IA&ACF will moderate all games. Players having rules or other
types of disagreements during the tournament will email a request for a
decision to the IA&ACF (also send a copy to your opponent). The word
"DECISION" (in caps) must appear in the message subject field. All
decisions will be final (no appeals).

Normally, when a decision is called for, the clock for both players will
be stopped. However, this procedure might also obviously be used as a
stalling tactic. If the IA&ACF feels that the question is invalid, or too
elementary in essence, the time will be charged to the player who made the
request for the decision.
