This is a description of a sample Zoid game for the purposes of explaining
the concepts encountered.

Figure 01 shows the first 6 moves made. This ascii representation makes
a poor substitute for the color graphics i was fortunate enough to have
when this game was played out. That is because i had the game printed out
out a piece of paper and had 4 colored pencils!

In the beginning, there is a basic land rush to stake out unclaimed hexagons
by spreading what meagre legs your opponent has given over as much space as
possible. Over the years it seems that the general principles i borrowed
from Go have borne out. The corners and edges are usually the best areas to
go for first. This may be related to the fact that a defensive move on the
outside can only make about half of its influence work. The rest would be
wasted off the edge of the board. So these first 6 moves are all in the
corners. Note that the pieces gradually get bigger, as the no-repeat rule
takes effect. The actual positioning in each corner is a pathetic attempt
to create good shape from future attack. It is a bit of a guess.

The current half-count of this position is 9 halves to 11 halves. At
this point the second player, Ezra, gives the first player, Olga, the X,
a 5-legged piece that only comes in contact with 4 hexagons. This is the
last piece that touches fewer than 5 hexagons. How does Olga play this?
She can play at the top left along the edge there, but then there is a lot
of concentration of her grubstakes on that side and Ezra could lay waste
to them for quite a number of turns. She could also squeeze it against
some # legs along the edge and get the full 4-count for it. But this is
the very beginning and there so much unclaimed territory out there....
