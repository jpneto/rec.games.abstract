Hello, everyone. I'm Mike Feir. There are a few reasons for writing this
article, not the least of which is to introduce myself. I've been blind since birth, and
have been using computers since around age ten. I'm goint into my final
year of a BA degree in university in September. Interactive fiction is one
of the most common type of game which is accessible to the blind, since it usually consists of plain text.
As a result of this, much of what I discuss in Audyssey, the magazine I edit which
deals with games accessible to the blind, is interactive fiction. Last July, I decided to start the magazine up so that I could share my knowledge
of accessible games with a larger community, and perhaps start a forum for discussion. The up-coming issue will include a letter and
article concerning the abundance of mazes in interactive fiction. Several complaints have come my
way about mazes, as blind people find it somewhat hard and tedious to deal with.
Braille graph-paper isn't exactly cheap, so it's harder to map out a maze for us. Personally, I simply use
a walkthrough if one is available to get through them, unless they are more than just mazes.
Another complaint which might interest you, and certainly surprised me, was that no one seems to
give much thought to exactly how much can be noticed via hearing. I hadn't even thought about that,
despite having played IF games for nearly half my life. The best use of hearing
in IF that I've found was in Sofar, in the cavern.
If anyone wants to make better use of hearing in a game, feel free to ask me any questions
you like. I'll be happy to render any assistance I can to writers of IF. Also, if any of you want
to write letters, announcements, articles, or previews for Audyssey, I'll be happy to include them. To subscribe to
Audyssey, available freely on the Internet, contact J.J. Meddaugh at:
jmeddaug@cris.com.
I find the conversation on this group most interesting, and plan to join in more
fully once this issue is published in around six days or so.
Until then, I'll be unable to read all of the articles, so don't be too surprised if I miss something.
Anyhow, I hope to get to know a lot of you better in the near future.
