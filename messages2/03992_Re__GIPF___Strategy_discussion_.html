OK. The first observation is that there are several levels of play.

In the basic game, players take turns sliding pieces onto the board. If
a player has no p[ieces left to add to the board, they lose. If a
player makes a line of 4 of their pieces, they remove all four pieces
back to their reserve, and any opponent's pieces that continue that line
are removed from the game.

In the standard game, each player starts with 3 Gipf-pieces; double
pieces, which need not be removed from the board when a player makes a
line of 4. Players may win in the same way as in the basic game *or* by
capturing the opponent's last Gipf piece.

The tournament game starts with a completely empty board - players take
turns to slide Gipf pieces onto the board until they decide to add their
first basic piece; after that, they can only add basic pieces.

If you aren't already playing the tournament version of the game, I
suggest that you start; in the basic game, each capture means that the
capturing player loses 4 pieces from the board, and has to rebuild their
position. This makes for quite a slow game. With the advent of the
Gipf pieces, players can capture *and* maintain a presence on the board.
Also, the second method of winning makes for deeper strategies, and
allows differing styles of play.

Anyway, on to strategy...

- if you have more pieces in your reserve than your opponent, you have
the option of disrupting his/her lines, and either winning when your
opponent can't make a line, or forcing your opponent to make a line at a
disadvantageous moment, i.e. when you will capture an opponent's piece
on your next move, and they can't stop you because they have to make a
line
- triangulate! If you just try building a line straight in to capture
an opponent's piece, then the opponent can slide in pieces from the
opposite side of the board to stop you - launch an attack from two sides
at once at the same piece, and aim for where the piece will be, not
where it is!
- remember that you can win by capturing your opponent's last Gipf
piece; use the threat of capture to force your opponent to remove
his/her Gipf pieces until there is only one left, then go for the kill -
be willing to sacrificea few pieces to do so, but keep an eye on your
reserve.

Well, that's a start...
