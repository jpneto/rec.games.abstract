I certainly don't know of one. Even though there are many restrictions
that one has to take into consideration, for any given position there
won't be too many that one has take into account, so it seems. But
automating all of it into a program to work for an arbitrary position is a
lot of work. Also, it's hard to define the criterion under which to
"stop" the search, i.e., when you know the position definitely is legal.

: Comparing the hardest retro-problem with the hardest "matt in n" problem ,
: what would n be ?

Depending on what "hard" means... there are mates in 270, etc., that have
been constructed. There are also positions that (supposedly, I haven't
checked it myself) requires 163 moves to reach from the opening
position. Also positions in which the last 54 (half-) moves are uniquely
determined.

: What amount of programming effort has to be made (e.g.compared to

: to have a program, that is able to solve any retro-problem
: in -say- some hours on a PC ?

: What do you estimate, when will we have such a program ? (if ever)

I think the first step will have to be to make a "catalog" of types of
known legal positions, to try to aim the search for these positions. (We
can stop once we reach those positions.) I don't know of any work in this
direction, but I think it will be needed. It just seems too hard to come
up with a general sufficient condition for legality.

Then one will have to write routines to rule out illegalities. I have
done some of this in a retro program I developed with a friend last year
(mentioned in an earlier post in this thread).

One difficult part that I
didn't do was handle all the information that could be drawn from
promotions. "Long-range" information (i.e. things that could not be
determined from a mere search) from promotions include things such as
which square(s) the promoted pawns could have come from, what path they
took to promote, what square they promoted on, how the promoted pieces
escaped from the promotion square, and whether the promoted piece is still
on the board or was captured (and where, etc...) This is probably the
area for the greatest potential improvement for Retractor.

Another difficult aspect is to handle information from captures. Counting
pawn captures is one thing (and not trivial!!) but then using
information regarding what _pieces_ were captured, etc., could also be
very important. And where those pieces were captured, etc.
All that would have to be stored as possible information.

Then there are some exceptional cases to consider, such as the class of
positions where the side to move is determined because the position
determines the parity of the number of moves made ("parity argument").

: What is the probability, that a random chess-position (satisying the

Ah, but here's the problem. Which constraints are "obvious" ? Is a
position with 8 white pawns on the board, with 2 of them on b2 and d2, and
a white bishop on h2, "obviously" illegal?
