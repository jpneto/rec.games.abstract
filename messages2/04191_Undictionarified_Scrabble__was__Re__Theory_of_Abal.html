Douglas Zare <zare@math.columbia.edu> writes:
> * Has anyone found a playable version of Scrabble which does not depend on a
> dictionary, e.g., if digits were used instead of letters and multiples of 11
> were used instead of words?

What a good idea! My girlfriend loves word games (Scrabble, Boggle, you
name it) and usually beats me convincingly at all of them. I would find
similar games that eliminate the dictionary far more elegant; being able
to consider the value of a move by studying the board position and some
simple rules alone is far more appealing than memorising a large number
of words.

I haven't tried playing the digits and multiples of 11 idea, but my
initial feeling is that since the frequency of valid numbers is just
as high as for large numbers as it is for small ones, the strategy
might vary considerably from Scrabble (not necessarily a bad idea of
course, but it might be worth trying to get closer). Offhand I can't
think of many simple sets which contain many small numbers and fewer
large ones; the primes perhaps, or Fibonacci numbers, but mentally
factoring 8 digit numbers isn't much fun and I can imagine arguments
over valid primes being worse than valid English words! How about
multiples of 11 where no digit appears more than once?

Now I come to think of it, Sid Sackson invented a game with a very vague
connection to Scrabble: Domino Bead Game (_A Gamut of Games_ 2nd ed.,
p. 172). "In the play of the game dominos will be placed next to
each other to form groups in a vertical and horizontal direction,
similar to the way words are formed in a crossword puzzle. Each group
must have a definite number pattern and a pattern is defined as soon as
the same number appears twice." A variation can be played with two
sets of dominos, starting with one each, no shuffling, and keeping all
dominos face up (for a deterministic, complete information game). I
imagine that these could be played on a Scrabble board with a little
effort.

I'd be interested to hear any other ideas.
