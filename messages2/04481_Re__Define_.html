On Mon, 16 Oct 2000, Nick Wedd wrote:

> You can play chess "blindfold". At least, if you are good enough you
> can. So, yes.

But most games could be played blindfolded, given sufficient memory and
spatial imagination. Most people don't have the memory needed to play
Axis & Allies without a board and pieces and a hit chart, but in theory,
it could be done.

The average person can't play chess blindfolded either. That some
extraordinary people can doesn't (in my opinion) make chess any more
abstract than A&A.

In fact, I find it very difficult to draw a clear line between abstract
and non-abstract games. Rather, there is a scale of abstractness, and
some things are more abstract than others, but all games are abstractions
to some extent, involving some sort of game mechanics designed to mimic
some action or effect.

How close these mechanics are to reality doesn't change the fact that
they are still abstractions. Thus, in my view, all games are abstract.

Most any "abstract" game can be assigned a theme (checkers simulates a
war, Go simulates amoebas, etc.), and most any themed game can have it's
theme stripped away, with circles and squares and funky symbols
representing all the themed elements. The fact that themed games like
Monopoly can have multiple themes supports this. The abstraction is the
common element between the various themed versions; the themes are just
icing to make the abstractions more palatable and easier to remember.
