> > Well, winner-takes all diplomacy provides a port sort of a large field
> > of competitors. However, I believe that diplomacy *is* individually
> > quantifiable. Each player in the game gets 1 point for each supply
> > center controlled at the end of the game, is one simple rule. Another
> > might be 1 point per supply center per turn of control. Both of these
> > rules provide a total ordering of the players, without dramatically
> > altering the game psychology (the core component of Diplomacy).
>
> Tournament Scoring systems have provided and continue to provide a fruitful
> basis for discussion among Diplomacy players. I am not aware of any that use
> "1 point for each supply center", however; and I believe such a scoring
> system would indeed alter the game psychology.

Sorry for speaking out of turn, then. I was unaware of *any* tournament
scoring systems. I was considering this primarily from the standpoint
of how to provide a total sort of the players in a tournament. I was
also looking for a simple rule to make a quick 'net point with.

> The rules give winning as the goal.

IIRC, the rulebook says 18 supply centers controlled determines a win.

> A Diplomacy tournament scored by centers
> is like a baseball tournament scored by base hits. Along with the conceptual
> problems are practical ones, involving players intentionally throwing games
> or delaying their own unstoppable victory.

Again IIRC, a coalition that controls 18 can also call a win. My first
scoring system does not discourage this style of "win". The second
one, as you have pointed out, change the game a bit. It was intended
to afford some points to players who were eliminated before the
winning coalition declares the end-of-game.

I'm not sure how terrible this is, though. The same skills are
tested regardless of the scoring system. They are built into the game
mechanics. Its rather like the difference between contract bridge and
duplicate. The game goals are a little bit different because of the
different scoring systems, but a good duplicate player is certainly
going to be a pretty good contract player (and vice-versa).
