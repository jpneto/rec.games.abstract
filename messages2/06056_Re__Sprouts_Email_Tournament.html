>> In sprouts, either you are able to analyse the position fully (when
>> either you know that you have a won position, or you know that your

>> can't and the move that you make might as well be random.
>
>Isn't this statement true of all abstract games, not just sprouts?
>(Note: this question is semi-rhetorical, in that I have my own reason
>why it is not even true for sprouts.)

It is true of all those CGT (Combinatorial Game Theory) games, such as
sprouts and nim, in which the values of all positions are stars. In
these games, the position is neutral between the players - all that
matters is which player's turn it is, and whether an odd or an even
number of moves will be made. The players, of course, can influence the
latter.

It is not true of chess, go, and most of the games discussed here (I see
Zhadu, Octo, Arimaa, and Dvonn at present). In these games, it is
possible to make what appears to be a gain, without achieving a position
that you can prove is a win.
