Clickomania is an intriguing puzzle game, for one player, which runs on
PCs. The game board consists in a NxM rectangular grid. Its squares are
colored, and there are significantly less colors than squares. A move
consists in pointing a connex pattern of identically colored squares
which encompasses at least 2 squares: the pattern disappears and the
columns of squares above it collapse, so as to fill the hole previously
occupied by the pattern.

Example:

TTFF FF
OOXI TXI F
OXXX TXXX TTFI
-> ->
(O) (X)

The goal of the game is to eliminate all of the colored squares.

As a cure to my own clickomania, I had a look at its mathematical side
(say, from a computer science point of view): I examined its
computational complexity - more precisely, that of the following
question: given an initial configuration, is there a solution (a series
of moves that lead to a blank rectangle) ?

I came up with the following results: clickomania is NP-complete, and so
are the 1-column (many colors) subcase and the 2-columns, 5-colours
subcase. On the other hand, the 1-column 2-colours subcase is tractable
(even though it's not that easy to prove it so !).
I'd like to know whether those problems have been studied before, and if
the status of the other subcases is known. Also, are there any sorts of
clickomania tournaments (for humans and/or for computers) ? Are there
any known good heuristics to play clickomania ?
