Score 4 (which is a commercial, probably trademarked, name for this
particular variant) has the added constraint that a piece cannot be
played unless all the spaces directly beneath it have been used.
I.e., it uses the physical constraint of gravity on the pieces in
each vertical line of four.

What Patterson described sounds closer to Qubic, a rather old 3-D
tic-tac-toe that indeed used 4 plastic planes separated by posts at
the corners. I believe Qubic differed from pure 3-D TTT by not
including all possible lines of 4. In particular, I think it DID
allow a diagonal on a single horizontal plane, but did not count
the diagonals on vertical planes. (I think it did count the "space
diagonal", i.e. the line of 4 that runs from one corner of the cube
to the unique opposite corner.)

As Ragnarsson says, 4x4x4 TTT has been solved. I think it turns out
to be a first-player win, and I _think_ the solution also applies to
Qubic and to Score 4.

For more information about the first-player win, try to find
Mathematics Magazine, vol 53 no 4, September 1980, pp. 202-216, for
Oren Patashnik's proof.
