1. chance: they flip a coin at the start to see who gets possession
first.

a bouncing football bounces in a way that cannot be
distinguished from having a random element.


2. guesswork: the coach sends in a play or the captain comes up
with one & they form a huddle to conceal the play
from the defense.


3. human imperfection: easily the most dominant element of luck
in a football game. how well you execute
the planned play or how well you stop it
is heavily dependent on player imperfections.


4. chaos: you have 22 players out there tripping & knocking each
other down. you have numerous occasions of the plan
breaking down & having to snatch order out of chaos.


football has all of the kinds of luck mentioned so far.....


a game of no luck that is not abstract? i'm having trouble with
that. i think that the term "abstract", when used in the looser sense
rather than the narrower sense of this usenet group, cannot be pried
out of the concept of a "game". even football with its gridlines
and clock can be imagined to have a transform that can move it out of
the real world into simulation space.
