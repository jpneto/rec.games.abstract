Matthew Daly (daly@PPD.Kodak.COM) wrote:

: How about this: (this will biased towards US residents, but you can
: adapt it to your surroundings)

: One player starts by naming a US state. The other player names a property
: that that state has (see below) and then a state that doesn't have that
: property. The first player then has to name a property that both of those
: states has and a state that doesn't have that new property. Play
: continues until one player cannot come up with a property for a set of
: states, at which the other player wins.

: Sample Game:

: 1: Washington
: 2: States that border the Pacific Ocean - Nevada
: 1: States in the Western Time Zone - Kentucky
: 2: States with one-word names - New York
: 1: States in the contiguous US - Hawaii
: Player 2 is stuck at this point, so player 1 wins.

Ah, but player 2 could respond with:

2: States with names which begin and end with different letters--
Alabama

: Of course, "property" needs to be defined. Without actually playing
: the game, I would require that at least two states meet the property
: and at least two states don't.

Certainly you'd want to define property to exclude ludicrous
properties. Player 2 above could have said "States with names containing
"a," "w," or "k"-- Ohio". Extending this principle, however, means that
each game could go on for a loooong time.

: -Matthew
