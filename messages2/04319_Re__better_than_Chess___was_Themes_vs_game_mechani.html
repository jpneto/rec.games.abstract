>On that note, I would consider the following games better than chess:
>- Go
>- Octi (beats chess on strategic depth and also simplicity of rules)
>- Grand Chess (the pieces result in a more logically symetric game)
>- Domination/Focus (supports 2-4 players equally as well)
>- Star (obscure game by the maker of Poly-Y)
>- Olix (Knizia's Pente derivative)

I agree with Go, though I find it dry and lacking in the personality that chess
has. I think Octi is too new to be sure about. Chess, after all, has hundreds
of years if playtesting behind it. Grand Chess is just one of very many
expanded chess variants, and agree that it's a worthy one, but I'm not
convinced that it's better than chess mostly because of the decreased unit
density, and again, it needs more time. And while I like both Focus and Star,
I doubt if either has the positional complexity and subtlety of Chess. Focus
in particular strikes me as a very calculation-based game whereas chess
requires a great deal of positional judgement. I don't know Olix, but I think
Pente is a good game, so I would probably like it.

Other games I would nominate for consideration, based on either elegance or
depth or both are:

-Shogi
-Chessgi (western chess play with shogi-like drops)
-Havannah (Christian Freeling's Hex-like connection game played on a hexagonal
board with three different winning geometries.)
-Emergo (also by Freeling: a checkers-like game where jumped pieces are trapped
beneath the jumping pieces can be later be freed.)
-The Game of the Amazons (a territory-making game with pieces that move and
then fire "arrows" that become impassible barriers.)

I don't claim that any of these are necessarily better than chess, just that
they are great games. And I could probably list quite a few more chess
variants that I think are at least as good as original chess, but that's
probably a whole new thread.
