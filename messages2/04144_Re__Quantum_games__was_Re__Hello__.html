This reminded me of bughouse chess, which requires 4 players, using 2 boards,
and 2 chess sets: http://www.bughousechess.com

One possibility for QM Chess is the "drop," using 2 6-sided dice (one green,
one red) to determine a new location within a 6x6 area chosen by the player
(one of 4 6x6 areas that include a corner of the board). The player would
choose one of his/her own pieces (except a pawn or the king), roll the dice,
and move the piece to the new location. Subsequent moves in a chain reaction
(described below) would still be in the same 6x6 area.

If the piece lands on any other piece, including an opponent's piece, that
would start a chain reaction: the same player would roll the dice again to
relocate the second piece, and so on until a piece lands on an unoccupied
sguare. If the dice have the piece landing on itself, then that piece is
removed from the board, and that ends that turn. Of course, that might mean,
on rare occasion, that a king is removed from the board, which would end the
game.

The "drop," and the resulting chain reaction, would be an optional move,
instead of a regular move. It isn't strictly quantum mechanics, just a
thought.
