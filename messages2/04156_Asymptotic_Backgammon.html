Asymptotic Backgammon

From: Douglas Zare <zare@math.columbia.edu>
Date: 27 08 1999 00:00:00 GMT

Consider a simplified form of backgammon where all checkers start on the
bar,
the doubling cube is not used, and the sides may not have the same
number of
checkers. If necessary, one can replace the dice with a coin labeled
1&2.

I'm curious what work others have done towards analyzing the winning
strategies and probabilities as the length of the board goes to
infinity. In
particular, even the n checkers against 1 variation seems interesting.

What types of strategies should one use in 2-1?
For which n does the probability of winning go to 1 as the board's
length tends
to infinity?
