The square is shown in 3 left columns of numbers; sums of row
elements appear in middle 6 columns; and sums of column elements
in left 6 columns. For example, sums for a row p q r are listed
in order p, p+q, p+q+r, q, q+r, r. The two repeated sums are
11 = 9+2 = 5+6 and 18 = 1+17 = 7+5+6.

The program, written in C and recursive to depth at most 28,
takes about 20 minutes to exhaustively test all possibilities.
There are 9 other #25 squares with 1 and 2 in the positions
shown, and all #25 squares have 1 and 2 in the middle cells
of adjacent sides, if my program is to be believed.
