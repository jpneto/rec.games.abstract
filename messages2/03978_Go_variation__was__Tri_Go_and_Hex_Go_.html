To continue this interesting thread, here's a go variation
I enjoy playing. I haven't found a name yet so if anybody
has an idea...

The game is played on a 19x19 board with the standard stones.
A new set of "neutral stone" is added (we used coins). A
predefined number of these stones is placed randomly on the
board (it can also be a mutual agreement, or a pre-game phase
where players place neutral stones)

What are these coins doing? They behave exactly like the
borders: they can be used to remove a liberty but do not
contribute to build a group of any color.

Why do I find this game interesting? Because it forces a lot
of reading; standard joseki does not hold; and all in all,
it brings up creative, unusual, and interesting play.

It can also be used as a way to narrow the gap between an
experienced player (who see patterns in standard go easily)
and a beginning one, who knows less tesujis but can read
well.

Finally, it's a good way to force reading.

If you have already tried this variation and enjoy it,
maybe we could find a way to play it over the net...
...or just to study it. (I believe eye patterns and
adapted tesuji can be found in the neighborhood of a coin)
