Bill Taylor wrote:
>
> The idea of simultaneous movement is a good one.
>
> It does, of course, *substantially* change the nature of the games involved,
> adding as it must do an element of incomplete information, but most of
> a games original features would still obtain.
>
> The question of what to do when the two revealed moves are inconsistent could
> be resolved in various ways. IMHO the best suggestion was that both those
> moves are forbidden while a simulataneous re-choice is made.

In (some) games where the only moves are piece placement, (go, hex,
go-moku, &c,) the conflict can be resolved by creating a new neutral
piece type that is placed at the position where two players
simultaneously move. This is the tact taken by a version of single
capture go I have become familiar with through Ackanomic. (rules are at
http://www.ackanomic.org/rules/rule1250.html#R1250.13) Simultaneous
moves to a position create a neutral stone that simply fills up a point,
(so that it cannot be counted as a liberty and no player can move to
that point again.) Go-moku might work fairly well with a similar rule.
For Hex I might prefer that a simultaneous move position should be
counted towards connections for both sides. Of course this doesn't work
if there are moves other than placement or conflicts other than
simultaneous placement at the same position.

> Chess is an interesting case - my preference for such things as whether
> castling over a new check was permitted, would be to always allow it.
> Similarly for blocked-checks or released-checks:- always deem that the move
> order was whichever would ALLOW the castling. Provided the two moves were
> otherwise consistent, of course.

There is also the interesting case of two kings simultaneously castling
to the same side, with the intervening files being open; whichever order
you choose, only the *first* move is legal, unless you deem the moves to
be actually simultaneous, in which case there is probably no problem. :^
