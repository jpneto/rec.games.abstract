Is it that easy? It's by no means clear to me that P-KB3 is such a bad
move that the advantage actually passes immediately to Black. It is
probably high on the list of questionable starts though. I think that
if you were playing White and I could choose your first move, I might
pick P-KR4.

Anyway I think the swap rule in chess is a kind of interesting
proposal. If it were adopted in serious high-level play, psychological
factors would be considered as well as objective ones. Sometimes, for
example, you may know that your opponent has made a deep study of
systems with P-Q4 but never plays P-K4. In that case you could try
P-K4 and present him with a dilemma: it's a good opening move but one
he doesn't know much about.

LT (a tournament chessplayer)

P.S. An alternative proposal for neutralizing the White advantage was
posted to the chess newsgroups a few months ago: simply award points
in a way that cancels out White's statistical advantage. For example:
White gets 10 points for a win, 4 for a draw, 0 for a loss; Black gets
11 points for a win, 5 for a draw, 1 for a loss.
