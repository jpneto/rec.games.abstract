Here is a scheme for chess encoding which uses 184 bits for a full, 32 piece
position.

Encode the board position as 64 bits, with 0=unoccupied, 1=occupied. Ignore
Kings.

Encode pieces next. 0=WR,1=WN,2=WB,3=WQ,4=WP,5=BR,6=BN,7=BB,8=BQ,9=BP. This
will produce a 30 digit number. This will require 100 binary bits, since
30 digits < 10^30 < 2^100.

Identify WK square (6 bits), the BK square (6 bits).

Next encode White's castling options: 00=no castling, 01=castling QS permitted,
10=castling KS permitted, 11=castling both sides permitted.

Next encode Black's castling options: 00=no castling, 01=castling QS permitted,
10=castling KS permitted, 11=castling both sides permitted.

Next encode the move. 0=White's move, 1= Black's move.

Next encode the square on which an e.p. capture can be made. This square
will be directly behind a pawn on the fourth rank of the player not to move.
Thus there at most 8 possible squares and this takes three bits. If e.p.
is not possible, select a theoretically impossible square (there must be
some of these).

Total = 64+100+6+6+2+2+1+3 = 184 bits.
