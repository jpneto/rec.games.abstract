>You forgot Calvinball. :-) I'm not sure where you're going with
>this. Different authors have different ways of categorizing games,
>but IMHO, there are still only 3 archtypes (race, acquisition,
>conflict). All of the games you mention, though some novel twists on
>mechanics may have been added, still fall into those three archtypes
>(and/or combinations of those archtypes).

Whew. Where to start?

It *may* be true that all existing games fall into three categories
(I'm not sure what you mean by "archetypes" in this context), but that
leaves out a heck of a lot of other useful categories. IMHO, your
system corresponds fairly exactly to saying that all human beings
should be seen as falling into only two categories -- male and
female. While this categorisation has demonstrable evolutionary value
:-), it leaves out far, far too much about the human condition. What
about children? Or old people? Or soldiers? What about people of
different nationalities? Or sexual orientations? Or personality types
(Myers-Briggs again)?

There are many, many *useful* categories of people that are orthogonal
to the male-female axis. Not to mention hermaphrodites and people with
other genetic variances from the usual XX/XY dichotomy -- there are
several kinds! I propose that saying the *only* useful categorisation
of games is "race/acquisition/conflict" is about as valid as saying
the *only* useful categorisation of people is "male/female" -- i.e.,
not at all. I further propose that even within the
"race/acquisition/conflict" category system there are probably a
number of games that do not fall within any of the three categories,
or blend two or more of them, or both.

I want to develop new categories and a new vocabulary for talking
about games. *That's* where I'm going with this.

BTW, could you please explain how in Bog's mighty name you can fit
Nomic under either "race", "acquisition", or "conflict"? It's *quite*
a stretch for any of them.

>> 2. Another interesting new category is the kind of game that centers
>> around making new rules, like Nomic, Fluxx, Das Regeln Wir Schon!, and
>> Mao. I call them "metagames". Are there any other names for them? What
>> do you think of the term "metagame"? And can you name any more games
>> in this category?

>Both breaking rules and changing rules are not new ideas. Many games
>have done it previously on a lesser scale (Uno, for one) with a card,
>a particular die roll, etc. Even Monopoly, with its "Do not pass Go,
>do not collect $200" falls into this change of rules niche.

Well, as I was pointing out the other day, the role of "It" in Tag
might be thought of as a special power a la Cosmic Encounter. The
genius of Cosmic Encounter lies in just how far it takes the special
power idea. And the genius of Nomic lies both in how far it takes the
rule-changing idea, and in that it lets players not only change rules,
but write them from the ground up and vote on them.

Again, how on Earth does an RPG fall within your three-category
system? (I'm sort of afraid to ask...)
