100 players, 100 games, 4.49 standard deviation, outliers of
62 and 39. Seems pretty good to me.

NCAA football usually produces a few teams with records in
the vicinity of 12-0, and a few with records in the vicinity
of 0-12. This is equivalent to 100 and 0, which is obviously
a much wider range than 62 and 39. It's very, very likely
that the 12-0 team should be ranked higher than the 0-12 team.

Can you argue that a 11-1 team should be ranked higher than a
12-0 team, e.g. on the basis that the 12-0 team played much
weaker opponents? Sure. Many people do. But, while actual
performance isn't a perfect measuring stick, what else are
you gonna use?

How does changing the number of players affect the standard
deviation? What about changing the number of games? What if
the 100 players' chances of winning are evenly distributed
from 70% to 30%, rather than all 100 players having 50%?
