|...
|A "bag" denotes here a finite collection of strictly positive integers.
|A bag is unordered and may contain several instances of a same integer. ...
|The bag product is defined as follows: given two bags A and B,
|- form their "cartesian product",
|- replace each pair of integers by the product of its two members.
|The bag you come up with is the product of A and B. ...

Let R be the polynomial ring Z[x1,x2,x3,...] of countably many independent
indeterminates over the integers, and let S be the subset of R, closed under
multiplication, consisting of polynomials with all coefficients nonnegative.
Then (as someone here pointed out earlier) multiplication of your bags
is isomorphic to multiplication in R, restricted to S; the isomorphism
is given by sending, for example, k x1^a x2^b x3^c into the size k bag
{2^a 3^b 5^c, 2^a 3^b 5^c, ..., 2^a 3^b 5^c}.

|My first question was about the unicity of [decomposition into prime factors]..
|Dean Hickerson came up with a family of counter-examples, according to
|the following pattern:
| {1,n} * {1,n^2,n^4} = {1,n^3} * {1,n,n^2} = {1,n,n^2,n^3,n^4,n^5}.

R has unique factorization into primes (irreducibles) in R, so a bag's
factorization into prime bags will come from lumping together elements of its
factorization in R. For example, the above factorization comes from the
prime factorization in R of 1+xi+xi^2+xi^3+xi^4+xi^5, which is
(1+xi+xi^2)(1+xi)(1-xi+xi^2). The last factor is not in S, but multiplying it
by either of the first two factors will put it in S, giving the factorizations
above.

| B.C1.C2... = D1.D2...
|The question whether a bag is avoidable is decidable (try all of the
|possible bags families until you stumble across a solution), so the next
|problem would be: is "unavoidability" decidable? What bags are in general

You mean `recursively enumerable', not `decidable'. Some prime bags
will also be prime in R; these will clearly be unavoidable. My guess is that
a prime bag which is not prime in R is avoidable. This would make both
avoidability and unavoidability decidable.

|...
|2) are there two distinct prime bags A and B, two integers n and m such that
| A^n = B^m ?

The polynomial f=3(1+x1+x1^3+x1^4)-x1^2 is prime in R, and A=f^2 and B=f^3 both
have all coefficients nonnegative; this gives an example of prime bags A and B
with A^3=B^2.
