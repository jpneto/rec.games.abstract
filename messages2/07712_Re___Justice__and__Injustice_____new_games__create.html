On Thu, 14 Jun 2007, Wlodzimierz Holsztynski wrote:

> My new invention, the games "Justice" and "Injustice", are on their
> surface just a variation of Nim. But the strategies are different, they
> need to be studied and the winning strategy has yet to be discovered.
>
> You start the game with any number of plates with (small :-) stones on
> them. A move consists of removing any positive number of stones from
> exactly one plate.
>
> JUSTICE: if after your move there is the same number of stones on each
> of the plates then you ** WIN ** (and your opponent loses).
>
> INJUSTICE: if after your move there is the same number of stones on each
> of the plates then your opponent wins, you ** LOSE **.

Justice is the game that's more similar to Nim. With an even number of
plates, I think that it yields to the same analysis as Nim. This is
because every losing position (equal stones on all plates) is a
first-player loss in Nim.

For an odd number of plates, that's not the case. For an odd number of
plates, n > 0 stones on each plate is a first-player win in Nim.
