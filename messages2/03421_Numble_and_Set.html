I wandered into the lounge of my dormitory today and saw a box in a stack
of board games that looked quite like my grandparents' Scrabble box, save
the fact that it was green instead of maroon. Curious, I wandered over
and discovered Numble, produced by Mag-Nif Inc. of Mentor, Ohio, and
copyrighted 1965. Tiles are numbered 0-9 and blanks (two blanks, four 0s,
five 9s, six 8s, ..., thirteen 1s), and play is on a 13x13 board with a
layout very similar to Scrabble (with double and triple number and
sequence spaces). A valid play is a nonascending or nondescending (i.e.,
descending or ascending with consecutive repitions of a number allowed)
sequences, the total of which (taking into account double and triple
number spaces, but not double and triple sequence spaces) is equivalent to
0 mod 3. I played one game tonight; our final score was 992 to 622 (it
scores much higher than Scrabble). It seems to play fairly well.

Anyway, I was wondering if anyone knew anything of this game. Is it still
being sold? If so, by who? If not, does anyone have a copy kicking
around that they'd be willing to part with for a reasonable sum? I'm
hooked, but I think it would be tacky to steal it from the dorm. :)

Also, while I'm posting, does anyone have an address I can write to to
order a copy of the game Set (with 81 cards, which you try to match up in
sets of three...)? There're no decent game distributors in town. Thanks
in advance!
