: >>5. Checkers, Draughts - Never been much of a fan of this group of games,
: >>as they seem to easily fall into patterns. I seem to remember reading
: >>once that in Checkers tournaments the players draw the opening three moves

: >Certainly with two very good players, draughts can get predictable, and

: >it enough as a teenager to get moderately good. Nowhere near tournament
: >standard - but good enough to give regular players a hard game, and win
: >once in a while.)

: >A friend of mine (who was very good at chess at the time - he played in
: >tournaments, and was far better than me) once made the mistake of rubbishing
: >draughts on the basis of reading something about the random openings used at
: >tournaments. He went on at great length about how simple and pointless the
: >game was. So I challenged him to a game...

: >Needless to say, I thrashed him. He agreed that perhaps it wasn't quite as
: >trivial a game as he first thought. Just because the experts find the game
: >predictable doesn't mean a non-expert will. Essentially, I think my advantage
: >was experience alone - I knew what certain positions would lead to, whereas
: >he had to figure out what would happen, and I knew how to tell a good position
: >from a poor one. My explicit lookahead was quite short - doubtless shorter
: >than his, as my brain doesn't work that way - but I had a much better
: >evaluation function.

: >It amused the hell out of me at the time.

But thats not really my point. If I am going to devote a substantial
amount of time to mastering a game, I don't want to find when I have
gotten there that I cannot beat players who are decent in the game with
out being given an opening at random.
