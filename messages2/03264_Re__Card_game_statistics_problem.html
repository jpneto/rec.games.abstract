This isn't quite right. If your opponent is looking for a specific
card (or combination of cards) then removing some of the cards - even
totally at random - makes them less likely to get it. If you pretend
that your opponent has an infinite supply of cards, with fixed
frequencies, then it makes no difference. Of course, you pay for
this as is you don't remove cards they're looking for you make them
come up sooner.

Now, as above, if this is Millstone in MtG you're asking about,
there's one point I'd add to the above: anyone can look at the
discards. Thus if you force your opponent to discard, both of you
gain information. If you don't know anything about their deck,
then this clearly helps you. If you do know what's in their deck,
both of you now have more precise information about what cards
your opponent is likely to get (less cards = less randomness). It's
not clear who this helps more - it depends on the situation.

All of the above is not nearly enough, however, to make Millstones
useful in an otherwise generic deck. It's a card you have to build
your deck around.
