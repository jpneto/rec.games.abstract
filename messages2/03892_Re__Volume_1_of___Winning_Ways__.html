madore@news.ens.fr (David Madore) makes the excellent point:

> There is one important difference here, at least: these books you
> mention are all now in the public domain, so one always has the
> possibility of borrowing the book from a library and having it
> entirely photocpied, or, if one is more ambitious, using ECR to
> produce a text file from them.

In other words, if someone really feels that Gauss' works ought to
be in print, no law is preventing him from publishing them.

I would like to see a detailed discussion of the costs of doing this
in the particular case of Gauss' Werke. Such a discussion can provide
a prototype for other ventures of this kind.

Presumably one can work from a microfilm. But even if one works from
the old volumes, generally 19th century materials don't scan very
well because of lots of broken letters and variations in the type.
It would probably require a team of volunteers to typeset the stuff
in TeX. I know how long it takes me to typeset something and I believe
that it wouldn't take that long to typeset all of Gauss' Werke with
a few dozen volunteers working on it. Checking it would be quite labor
intensive too.

There are a lot of options for storage and distribution, as well as
for ownership of the edition.

I'd really like to see all the details on paper.
