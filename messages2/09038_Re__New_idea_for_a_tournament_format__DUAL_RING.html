Rich Hutnik wrote:
> In short, a player plays two games at same time (one of each color),
> until they lose a game, and then they are eliminated. Players play until two
> players are left, and then the player with most wins, wins the
> tournament.

> Players tying can be handled one of several ways. One way is to have
> players replay the tie until there is a winner, or one of two players
> is eliminated by an opponent. The other way to handle a tie is to have
> the tie eliminate both players.

Interesting. A few things strike me.

One is that it would be possible for one individual to play many games and have
none of them come to conclusion because their opponents keep losing in their
other simultaneous match. I would imagine it might be frustrating to have your
game at an interesting point, and then it is called off and a new one set up
with a new opponent.

When down to the final three, it seems very open to manipulation. If two players
agree to stall on their common board (making quick non-threatening moves to save
time and brain power), they can likely force the third player out on grounds of
time or by direct win.

The way of handling ties is likely to be critical, especially as in chess they
seem relatively common. Letting the tie stand is not much good as it again seems
to permit alliances, where two players exchange down to a tie, so as to
concentrate on eliminating their other opponent.

It is basically a different 'game' from standard FIDE chess just through having
to play two sides at once. This may make rule changes like "tie = elimination"
more acceptable, but I can't see regular chess tournaments jumping to use this
format.
