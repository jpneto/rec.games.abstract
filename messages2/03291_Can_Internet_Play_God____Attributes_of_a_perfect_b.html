Can Internet Play God? - Attributes of a perfect board game (Summary)

From: Dr Bronia Grycz <destiny@gmtnet.co.uk>
Date: 25 05 1996 00:00:00 GMT




OVERVIEW


At the beginning of this discussion I asked:

Q1: Does anybody there know about a perfect board game?
Q2 What the essential attributes of such game would be?

The answers to Q1 were summarised in my previous posting.
The time-tested games of "Go" and "Chess" came top of the
league.

Many respondents observed that Q1 could not be confidently
answered without defining the meaning of *perfect* first.
A possible way of doing this is to list the essential
attributes of a *perfect* game, i.e.to answer Question Q2.

So which are the attributes?

Over 40 different replies were obtained which could broadly
be arrangrd in three categories: subjective (S), objective (O)
and practical)P) attributes.Out of these I have extracted
ten which I felt reflected best the spirit of the discussion.


RESULTS

In a nutshell, a *perfect* board game ought to:


A1 provide total enjoyment and esthetic experience (S)
A2 provide challenge and satisfaction (S)
A3 have simple, clear and elegant rules with minimal
exceptions (O)
A4 have elegant strategy, preferably not traceable
by a computer (O)
A5 give all players the same chance of winning but have
a clearly defined winner (O)
A6 symbolically reflect salient aspects of human life
and human interactions (O)
A7 have few, if any, imperfections ,and wthstand the
test of time (O)
A8 be highly playable, user-friendly and take reasonable
time to complete (P)
A9 require the balance of intelligence and/or wisdom
and/or social skill to win it (P)
A10 teach people something (P)


There were also sceptics who doubted that *perfect* could
be defined and others who believed that perfection might
not be achievable nor even desirable.


POSTCRIPT

To treat the subject rigorously, one would have to satisfy o
neself that the above attributes are fully representative,
complete, independent from one another and appropriately
weighted,

As the above attributes are *fuzzy* quantities, fuzzy logic
would have to be applied to evaluate each candidate for a
*perfect* game.Delphi consensus technique would be used to
ascribe these *fuzzy* quantities.Perhaps gamers with
sufficient mathematical knowledge could advise on this.

However, until this is done the sceptics may have a point
and the conclusion that "Go" and "Chess" are the nearest to
perfection, may be adopted as *the best we can do* answer
to Question 1.
