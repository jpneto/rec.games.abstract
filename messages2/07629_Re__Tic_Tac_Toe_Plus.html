At one time I wondered why so many people released their own versions of
tic tac toe into the world (or noughts & crosses as I usually call it).
It was some time later, as a budding writer of computer games, with a
catalogue of too-ambitious half-finished projects, that I figured out
why.

You do right to start simple, though there is, to be honest, a limit to
how simple one can go without writing something that nobody will play.
Might I make a suggestion? With your modifications to tic tac toe,
you're inventing a game as well as trying to code its AI. If you look
at a book like Murray's History of Board-Games Other than Chess, or
Bell's Board and Table Games from Many Civilizations, you'll find a
great variety of very simple games that are much more playable than tic
tac toe, whose strategies are already known, and which may generate a
bit more interest. Three men's morris, four field kono, and mu-torere
are examples I can think of without getting the books out. You'll find
information about these on the internet too.

But If you've put a lot of work into your tic tac toe game, then you'll
probably want to see it through. In that case, good luck to you, I hope
you have fun with it and that it leads you to more interesting projects.
