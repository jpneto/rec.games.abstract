Here is another problem inspired by the african game called "Awele".

Consider a finite array (vector) A of n buckets, and a finite set of
m identical tokens distributed in the buckets (a box may contain any
number of tokens). A move consists in taking the tokens from one of
the buckets and redistributing them, one token at a time, into the buckets
following the emptied bucket (starting with the leftmost of those buckets,
and proceeding towards the rightmost). If there are any tokens left after
the last bucket is fed, those tokens are taken out of the board; their
number is called the value of the move (if no tokens are taken out of the
board, the value of the move is zero).

Now let there be a series of values V1, ..., Vk of which sum is m.
- Is there a series of moves that yields that series of values?
- What if zero-values are not taken into account?

What is the status of the above problem - is it difficult? NP-complete?

[NB:

Let B1, ..., Bn be the numbers of tokens in the buckets, and the following
potential function:

f(A) = Sum Sum (n-i-j+2)
i in 1..n j in 1..min(Bn, n-i+1)

It is easily proven that:
1) f(A) = 0 iff A is empty;
2) any move (involving a non empty bucket) causes f to decrease by at
least 1;
3) playing the rightmost non empty bucket causes f to decrease by 1 exactly;
4) f can be polynomially bounded by the "length" of the problem
(by a polynom in m and n).

1) & 2) => any series of non trivial moves (moves on non empty buckets)
eventually empties the array.

4) => a series of moves which empties the array has a length polynomially
bounded by the length of the problem, so that problem belongs to NP.

1), 2), 3) => f(A) is actually the length of the longest series
of non trivial moves that can be played on A. Always playing the rightmost
bucket enables one to empty the array "as slowly as possible".]
