Hmmm.... I've actually seen this type of game in the arcades and bars,
In the arcade there was a game called ATAXX which used the same
principle of game play, except on squares instead of hexagons. It was
pretty neat, and somewhat addictive, as you won or lost against several
different levels of computer opponent, or another human player, it kept
track of your rank and win loss record. You gained more rank points by
playing the toughest computer player, less against the easy computer
player, etc. If time ran out you lost. (A good strategy to keep you
pumping in the quarters) The hardest computer player was very difficult
to beat consistently. KLOV link below... (The KLOV description is pretty
weak) I guess it didn't survive though, not enough violence or gore, and
it used more brainpower than trigger finger. *sigh*

http://www.tc.umn.edu/~johns594/arcade/klov/A/Ataxx.html

Also, in the bars, there are these "MegaTouch" table top coin op games
with a touch screen, you can choose to play this type of "Hexxagon"
game. The computer opponent in this game is easy to beat.

One good strategy is to try and get the opponent "penned" in, so that
his only move is to jump his piece across your line, and you can take
the spot he vacated, hopefully to gain several pieces, and better positioning.

That is it from me, my thanks to Bill Taylor for reminding me of ATAXX,
which I played at the college snack bar while waiting for my order!!!

Adam.

> Hexxagon is a game seemingly invented by, and distributed as shareware by,
> "Software Creations" of Maine.
>
> It seems like a quite good game. Played on a board of hexagonal cells,
> (with the option of starting with some missing), and a starting position
> (also optional) of standardly 3 pieces each alternately round the corners,
> it goes as follows.
> __
> __/ x\__
> __/ \__/ \__
> __/ \__/ \__/ \__
> __/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \__
> / o\__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ o\
> \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/
> / \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \
> \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/
> / \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \
> \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ Board showing usual starting position.
> / \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \
> \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/
> / x\__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ x\
> \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/
> \__/ \__/ \__/ \__/
> \__/ \__/ \__/
> \__/ o\__/
> \__/
>
> Each move can *either* be:
>
> A JUMP, where you jump one of your pieces to an empty cell 2 spaces away
> (linearly or knight-movely), regardless of what's in between, and then
> recolor all opposition neighboring pieces to the new cell into your color.
>
> *or*
>
> An OOZE, where you ooze into an empty cell adjacent to one of your own cells,
> leaving your old cell as is, and recoloring other cells as for a jump.
>
> It's a remarkably good tactical game, with some (but not too many)
> strategic ideas as well. It's strategic enough that we can easily beat
> our computer, though it doesn't actually seem to play with all that much
> tactical nous. I suspect a human expert could beat a really good program,
> but only with considerable difficulty.
>
> Anyway, I'm writing in to see if anyone else knows of it, and especially,
> to hear if anyone knows any good tactical or strategic concepts to bear
> in mind while playing it.
>
> Hopefully so.
