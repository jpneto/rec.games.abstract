> for 100 steps and counting the live pieces off the top versus the sides

In Wari (and a host of other Mancala games I do not know about) the
pieces are only distinguished by on whose side of the board they are. It
is debatable whether you play them one at a time or not, though.

A possible game idea (maybe done before):

Take a grid of NxN and a supply of stones to place on it. The players
alternate placing one stone on any free space on the grid. For every
square completed by the player he gains one point. First to gain M points
wins the game.

A square is any possible configuration of four stones that are placed so
that they are at the corners of a square. Examples of squares:

. o o .
. . . .

. . . .

o . . o

. o . .
. . . o
o . . .
. . o .

The obvious clarifications:

The grid should be made of squares too. You only gain points for the
squares that you complete with your current play.

I guess this could at least interest the occasional mathematician :)
