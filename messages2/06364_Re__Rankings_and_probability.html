I think you're missing the original poster's point. Even when there's no
skill involved, you get so much spread due to luck. Though an individual
game may have no luck, even chess has "luck" in that a player may have a
good game or not. With a perfectly matched set of chess players, you would
get approximately as much spread as if you were flipping a coin. (You would
get a little less spread, due to the fact that White has an advantage,
pushing everybody toward an even score.)

To conclude that there are differences in skill level, you need to show a
_greater_ spread in results than would be obtained from a game of pure
chance.
