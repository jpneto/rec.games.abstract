Yes, where the center of the board is occupied by the most powerful
player, but the next level down has more players. And each ring down the
hill has more and more players. Moves are made in their own 'event
horizon' if you will. Moves that are too far away to affect you won't
be waited for.

>
> Right again. Persistance should not inlcude (IMHO) stats that make you
> better than another player. If you are a better player, you should be able
> to prove it, and not rely on pumped up stats.

Personally I prefer abstract games where every piece is identical in
abilities, so call me a purist. So at the beginning of the game every
one starts with a set number of pieces and can chose where to be placed
on the hill. Pieces can be acquired by defeating opponents and higher
levels can be challenged when the player sees they are ready. Players
near or at the top will have more opponents so their pieces will be
under more threats. Alliances, treaties, violent skirmishes, betrayal!
now I'M rambling.

As with any abstract game balances will need to be experimented with.
And as far as leaving is concerned it needs to be decided what happens
to the pieces.

An other inspiration hit my while spell checking. I started disliking
the un-uniformity of the universe. The game can be territorial with no
center. That way there becomes a balance of number of pieces, stacked
out claim, and player abilities. Then it occurred to me that this could
quickly explode to very large proportions, diluting the interest. So
what if there was a gradual slope in value of land. Back to the hill
idea only more gradually sloped. The areas closer to the peek would be
more worth keeping.

Whadyathink?
