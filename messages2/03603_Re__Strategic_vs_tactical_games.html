apardon@rc4.vub.ac.be (Antoon Pardon) writes:
>Could someone explain me how to people come to evaluate a game as
>more tactical vs more strategic? What are features of both
>classes of games?

Well, when I play chess, I am an excellent tactical player; I
can see many winning combinations, I am pretty good at setting
up traps, avoiding them, and faking them. On the other hand,
I am a very poor strategic player; I never know if I want
to attack the king-side or queenside, I never quite know
which pieces to develop, and I don't understnad openings.

A rule of thumb is:
If you're asking yourself, "What's the best move?", you're
playing tactically.
If you're asking yourself, "Where's the best move?", you're
playing strategically.

Tactics refer to local exchanges. They are like the parry and attack
of a swordfight, like the individual skirmishes on a battlefield.
Strategy refers to a "whole-game" view. They are like the preparation
and study of a swordfight, and are like the deployment on a
battlefield.

In tactics, the individual move is important; in strategy, the
overall effect of the moves are important.

Computers are good at tactics, because they involve seeing
the moves immediately into the future, and one mistake or
forgotten possibility could completely change the outcome.
Precision is very important.

Computers are poor at strategy, because it involves seeing
very far into the future, and mistakes are fine, but its
important to know where you're trying to drive the game.
Accuracy is important; you don't want to be concentrating
on a section that's not essential to winning.

Hope this gives a good contrast.
