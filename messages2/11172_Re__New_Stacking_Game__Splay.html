I haven't played it yet, but I wouldn't jump to conclusions on the
basis of what the computer makes of it. In tactical terms, causing
opponent's stacks to collapse seems quite dramatic. But it requires
careful strategy to get to a position to be able to execute it.

Flags may be permanent, but so are live groups in Go. Nevertheless I
see you may have a point, though I can't really tell yet how serious
the 'lack of dynamics' will turn out to be. It may well turn out to be
a matter of taste. All I said about Splay could have been said about
my own game Symple: elegant, intricate, solid, new. But Symple lacks
drama, possibly more so than Splay. It's like a big ship heeling
slowly one way or the other, and by the time you get a clear picture
of which way, it's too late to do much about it.

Games like that require a strategist who likes to look deep into the
opening arrangements to harvest small advantages in the end. And small
advantages yet win the game, in the end.
