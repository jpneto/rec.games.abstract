>Unfortunately, the page does not describe the rules is perfect detail.
>I'm wondering what happens to the columns after they have been
>involved in a battle. Presumably they stay on the board.

Yes, they stay on the board. No piece is removed during the game.

>This would
>cause situations where three or more columns reside on one line. How
>do we decide what columns to compare? Do we compare all combinations
>or just the ones that have been added on the latest turn?

If you have two columns on a line on your side of the board, only the
strongest one is compared with the strongest one on the same line on the
opponent's side of the board.

(Since English is not my native language, is my explanation clear enough?)

>Are past
>victories recorded, or is the goal to have seven wins on the board
>simultaneously?


The goal is to have seven wins simultaneously. There are 13 contended lines,
so the the game is won when seven lines are conquered.
