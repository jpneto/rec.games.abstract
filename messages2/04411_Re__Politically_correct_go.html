Douglas Zare <zare@math.columbia.edu> writes:

| Steve Taylor mentioned the idea of politically correct go: each
| player plays gray stones. Any suggestions for a balanced game in
| which there is only one type of piece, played one at a time?

Sure. Take your favorite standard two-player game, and allow each
player to move on either side of the standard game. This is quite
different from just making all pieces the same color.

For example, for "Impartial Go", either player could place either a
black stone or a white stone on each turn, capturing stones of the
opposite color (so "atari" means "Merry Xmas!"), and the winner is the
person who captured the most stones when the game ends. I imagine
endgame play would be VERY different from standard go. Maybe the best
strategy is to play monochromatically. Then again, maybe not.

In Impartial Chess, either player can move with a piece of either
color, including captures and so on. If a turn begins with a king in
check, that king must not be in check when the move ends. (This does
NOT mean that the checked king's color must move next.) Non-capturing
Non-pawn Moves CAN be immediately undone, but repeating any position
three times is a draw. Whoever checkmates a king first wins. [This
must already have a name, but I can't find it on Hans Bodlaender's
excellent "Chess Variants" web page.]

In Impartial Othello, either player can place a piece of either color
and flip pieces of the opposite color. The winner is determined by
the color of the last piece. So if there are more white pieces, and
the last player played white, they win. Sounds trivial, but maybe it
isn't; I can easily imagine endgames where only the only legal moves
use the losing color.
