I was finding creative ways to waste time this evening and came up
with a game you can play with Legos. I'm not sure if it's any fun to
play yet, but it seems like it could be.

If anyone in the Seattle area feels like playtesting it with me, let
me know. I'll probably head down to Seattle Metro Gamers this weekend
and see if I can find a few guinea pigs. Also any comments would be
interesting.

RULES FOR PILLARS
(Warning--this may be a horribly boring game! I haven't tested the
rules.)

You need 2 to 4 players and a suitable inventory of Legos. All the
pieces described here are one unit thick except for the flat
baseboard. Pieces needed:

1 - 32 x 32 baseboard.
2 - 1 x 8 blue bricks
6 - 1 x 6 blue bricks
11 - 1 x 4 blue bricks
6 - 1 x 3 blue bricks
9 - 1 x 2 blue bricks
10 - 1 x 1 blue bricks

You need equal numbers of the same types of bricks in red, white, and
yellow.

If you get the 400 piece bucket of basic pieces (#1867), I believe you
should have everything you need except for the baseboard. The exact
number of pieces of each type doesn't matter as long as each player
has the same amount of each type.

The object is to build over the top of "pillars" that are placed on
the board. You will have to construct your bricks in three dimensions
so that they span as many pillars of your color as possible. You can
also gain an advantage by creating obstacles to prevent your opponents
from building over their pillars.

SETUP STAGE

Distribute bricks to players--one color for each player. If there are
only two or three players then just use two or three colors.

Make four pillars for each color. The first pillar is just a single 1
x 1 brick. The second pillar is two 1 x 1 bricks stacked together.
The third pillar is three 1 x 1 bricks stacked together. The fourth
pillar is four 1 x 1 bricks stacked together.

Each player passes his four pillars clockwise so that all players end
up possessing pillars of another player's color.

Players each roll a die and the highest roll goes first.

On a player's turn in this stage, he places any of his four pillars
onto any square on the board. After he has placed a pillar, his turn
is complete and the clockwise player begins his turn. After four
rounds, all of the pillars will be placed on the board, and the main
stage begins.

MAIN STAGE - FIRST ROUND

The player who went first in the setup stage, takes his turn first in
this stage. In the first round, he must place one brick on the board
in accordance with the following rules:

- The brick is placed on the baseboard. (As opposed to placing it on
top of other bricks).

- At least one square of the brick must be placed on the perimeter of
the baseboard.

After the player has placed one brick, his turn is complete and the
clockwise player begins his turn.

MAIN STAGE - SUBSEQUENT ROUNDS

On a player's subsequent turns, he again places one brick on the
board, but follows different rules:

- The brick must be placed so that it is adjacent to another brick of
the player's color. To achieve this, a brick may be placed next to,
above, or below another brick of the same color. A pillar of a
player's color may be used as an adjacent brick, but only if the
player has already built over the top of it.

- A brick must be "supported" on two of its squares. If either the
top or bottom of a square of the placed brick is joined to another
brick or baseboard, that square is said to be supported. It is legal
to use other player's bricks to support your bricks. It is legal to
place bricks that hang past the edge of the baseboard.

- A brick may not be placed over a pillar of another player's color,
even if it is not joined to it.

- A brick may not be placed in a way that *requires* removal of other
bricks to place it.

After the player has placed one brick, his turn is complete and the
clockwise player begins his turn.

END OF GAME

If a player can't place a brick on his turn, he is removed from the
game and loses. (This is an unlikely but possible condition.)

If one player builds over all four pillars of his color, he wins with
a score of ten. Other players' scores are calculated by what pillars
they've built over at this point. The pillar four bricks high is
worth four points, three bricks high is worth three points, etc.

If all bricks are used but no player has built over all four pillars
of his color, then the game ends and players are scored in the same
way as above. Highest score wins.
