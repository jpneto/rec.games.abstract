The reason why you care about both is that backgammon has more people
playing it that just about all the other abstract modern strategy
games you want to have as part of IAGO combined. It is abstract in
nature and a strategy game, and is about to hit a tipping point.
Boardgame Geek classifies it as an abstract strategy game also. Games
100 also have voted Backgammon variants on their Games 100 list.

> Here's the beauty of keeping these two objective criteria intact:

What I can argue here as a compromise spot is that backgammon fits in
as the exception to the rule in the classic games, and is the only
game with rolling of dice allowed in IAGO. Also, the world's earliest
version of chess, Chaturang, involves the rolling of dice to determine
what pieces to move. It is on the chess variants website also:
http://www.chessvariants.org/historic.dir/chaturang4.html

Also chess, prior to advent of the modern queen, had people playing
around with a use of a die to determine which pieces move.

While it is great to try to knock everything out in one swoop, it ends
up not accurately reflecting what is there and excludes some major
games. What you can do is have the Fortuna category I added be just
for the classics, with Kriegspiel and Backgammon as the only two games
in there. Perhaps have minor variants on other chess games. And cap
it off. The category is just for classics and not modern/standard.
This is a compromise position I can live with. The Fortuna category
isn't expected to be played by a lot of people, but should help out
here.
