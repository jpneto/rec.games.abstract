I wrote a Kriegspiel referee a few years earlier than that in JOSS, the
remarkably cool on-line system at RAND... it would have been about 1968,
just after we moved the system from our vacuum-tube Johnniac machine to
the incredibly fast and wondrous PDP-6. I don't have the reference to my
paper handy, but can dig it up on request.

At RAND we always used two boards and a single screen between them, and
the referee would integrate them mentally. Our rules were that the referee
announces:

- white/black to move
- pawn gone at <square>
- piece gone at <square>
- try at <square> [<square> ...]
- double try at <square> [<square> ...]
- check by a knight
- check vertical
- check horizontal
- check on the long/short diagonal
- no
- hell no

The "try" means the player can capture something with a pawn on a particular
square, so that if the player is in check and taking something there would
not relieve check, the illegal pawn capture would not be announced as a "try".
"Double try" means two pawns can capture there. The square announced is the
one to which the pawn would go, so I suppose a single pawn move of P-K4 (yes,
we used English notation) could result in "Double tries at K5 and K6."
All tries are announced on each move as long as they're available.

"No" means you can't move where you tried to move, and "hell no" means the
move you tried is obviously illegal, where the latter is somewhat subjective.
For example, if you try moving your a1 rook to a4 and get a "no", many
refs would say "hell no" if you then tried moving it to a5. All refs would
say "hell no" if you tried moving it to b2 to pad your "no" count to try to
convince your opponent you've found something interesting.

Other variations of Kriegspiel include not announcing potential pawn tries
(so that you have to check every one you're willing to capture every time);
not announcing what direction your check is coming from; and not announcing
whether something captured is a piece or pawn. Naturally these are all
inferior to our rules.

Implementing a netted ref a la ICS or IGS is problematical, since one of
the most interesting aspects of Kriegspiel is kibitzing; but since many
people are quite willing to cheat in both ICS and IGS, nothing could stop
them from coming in on a second channel as an observer. Eliminating
kibitzers altogether loses much of the fun, though. In my JOSS referee I
didn't have a kibitz option.
