On 22 Jul 2000 telford@xenon.triode.net.au wrote:

>But knowing how much to trust your own prediction is important too.
>You cannot ever be sure your predictor is right but you can get some
>indication as to how well you understand your particular opponent
>and you should make use of this information.

Precisely. If you have only one model, there's not much you can do.
But if you have a family of models, you can put a probability distribution
over them as well, and use Baye's theorem to update your a priori
estimate of your confidence in the various models as the data comes in.
I think I'd rather avoid augmenting a single predictor with a few
ad-hoc heuristics ;^)
