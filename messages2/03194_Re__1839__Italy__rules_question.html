|> When two major corporations merge, one of the constraints is that each
|> must have "performed at least one Operating Round". We have always

|> its first OR, provided that the partner has operated at some time.
|> Rereading the rules yesterday, I grew doubtful that this was the
|> correct interpretation.
|>

I believe you are correct to be doubtful; the company must have done a full
operating round to merge, so can't perform the merger itself until it's
second full OR (although another company could merge with it anytime after
it had performed a full OR). Or it least, this is the literal reading, which
is probably the way to go until Federico says otherwise. After all, if you
haven't completed the finincial step, you haven't performed a full OR.

The remember the practical upshot is that a start-up company cannot
immediately merge; it has to go through a train purchase phase before
it can merge. Annoying but probably quite reasonable from a game-play
standpoint.
