Call For Papers:
Machine Learning in Game Playing

(http://www.ai.univie.ac.at/icml-99-ws-games/)


------------------------------------------------------------------------

A Workshop at the
16th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML-99)
Hotel Park, Bled, Slovenia
June 30th, 1999


------------------------------------------------------------------------

During the last decade, the interest of the game-playing community in
machine-learning techniques has been growing significantly. While some
of
the main results (such as Tesauro's Backgammon program) are well-known
to
machine-learning researchers, we feel that the new challenges and
problem
statements posed by the game-playing research are still underestimated.
New
questions spawn innovative ideas that lead to novel algorithms while
deepening our understanding of the generic learning paradigms
(supervised,
unsupervised, and reinforcement) and of the intricate ways in which they
are
interrelated.

The objective of this workshop is therefore to bring together machine
learning specialists with scientists that have extensive experience in
game
programing. The mission is to help familiarize the machine-learning
community with the specific problems posed by games, to spell out main
issues that deserve further attention, and to promote further research
along
these lines. The following well-established researchers will deliver
invited
talks that will serve as the skeleton for research-paper sessions:

* Donald Michie and Jean Hayes Michie, University of Edinburgh, UK
Acquisition and Structure of Expertise in Two-Person Games

* Takuya Kojima, NTT Communication Science Labs, Atsugi, Japan
Knowledge Acquisition from Game Records

* Jonathan Baxter, Australian National University, Canberra,
Australia
(title to be announced)

* Michael Buro, NEC Research Institute, Princeton, NJ, USA
Is one neuron really enough to play games at Wordchampion-level?

* Jonathan Schaeffer, University of Alberta, Edmonton, Canada
(title to be announced)

In addition, we would welcome contributions by participants (see
submission
guidelines below).

The research papers and, if possible, also the invited papers, will be
published as Workshop Notes to be distributed among the participants of
the
workshop. Depending on the number and quality of submissions, it is our
intention to ask the authors of the most interesting papers to write
full-length versions to be published in a special issue of an
appropriate
journal or as a book (possibly in the series ADVANCES IN COMPUTATION:
Theory
and Practice of Nova Science Publishers).

We also encourage researchers to offer demos of their systems. Please
indicate, with the submission of your paper, whether you will be
interested
in giving a demo and what hardware requirements you have. Presenting a
paper
is not a prerequisite for a demo.

Submission Guidelines

Submissions should be sent to one of the organizers by April 12, 1999,
preferably in electronic form as a PostScript file. The length and
format of
the submissions should follow the ICML-99 recommendations. If you use
LATEX,
you may want to use this style file. The papers will be peer reviewed,
and
the authors will be notified about the acceptance or rejection by April
30,
1999. Camera-ready versions (formatted in ICML-99 proceedings style) are
due
May 14, 1999.

Attendance

Attendance, of course, is not limited to paper authors. In fact, one of
the
objectives of the workshop is to familiarize people from other areas of
Machine Learning with some of the work and important research issues in
Game
Playing.

However, in order to get an early estimate of the possible attendance,
we
would appreciate an informal note whether you would like to attend the
workshop (and, maybe, whether you intend to submit a paper and/or give a
demo). Of course, by doing so, you make no commitment whatsoever.
