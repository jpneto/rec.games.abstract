Both sound a bit arbitrary and/or restrictive. Surely there's a much
simpler hack available:

---------
A move consists in naming an point, then coloring (in your color)
ANY UNCOLORED SUBSET of the unit circle centred on your point.
---------

This would allow every game to be win-completable, "cross-cuts" like the
above would be linkable by a long line in Twixt-like fashion. Most moves
would still be full circles, of course, WLOG.


About REAL-HEX in general. This gets suggested from time to time, and with
the above rule (or similar) it sounds like a good game, especially with the
extra-distance handicap.

Except for one thing. Being a real-valued game, it might admit of rather
nasty arguments (even though the logic of the rules may be impeccable). Of
course, you could play it with a computer referee; but this would amount to
(most likely) actually playing it on a grid of 400x600 pixels with 20 diameter
circles, or thereabouts. So.... why not just do this ourselves!


Play APPROXIMATELY REAL HEX: on a 400-sided hexagonal rhombus, with each
move being: coloring a collection of (in fact all the) uncolored hexes that
lie within a 10-a-side (circle-shaped) hexagon of hexcells.
The exact numbers, 400, 10, could be adjustable to taste.

And even better for the handicap - keep the rhombus fully symmetric, but
just make it tha the opening move must be a 5-a-side hex-circle!


It's beginning to sound like a real goer to me to, now!
(Or should I say, an approximately real goer...)
