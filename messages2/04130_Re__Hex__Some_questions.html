Bridgit, a.k.a. Connexxions or Gale, is actually played on two square
lattices superimposed, like the stars on the US flag. Each player
plays horizontal or verical connections on his/her specific square
lattice, which also block the opponent's possible connections on the
other lattice. Moves are not made on the points of either lattice, but
between two points of your lattice. This is actually isomorphic to a
single square lattice, where each move IS on a lattice point, but the
rules regarding whether two diagonally adjacent tokens of your color
connect or not, depend on where you are in the lattice. Also, the grid
itself has the shape of a diamond, with diagonal sides relative to the
direction of "horizontal" and "vertical" within the lattice itself.

BTW, Mr. Wolff is correct that Bridgit has been solved. I can send a
solution file to anyone who asks.

"Hex on a square lattice" is ambiguous. I assume each move consists of
placing one token on a vacant lattice point. But are diagonally
adjacent tokens of the same color connected or not? If they are in all
cases, black & white paths can cross each other, and the game is more
a race game than a connection game. The first player certainly has a
win here. If diagonal connections are always true in one diagonal
direction but never in the other, this is the same as Hex, of course.
Bridgit corresponds to a more complicated situation regarding diagonal
connections, but we wind up with a simpler game, as frequently happens
when you mess with the rules of an abstract game.

If diagonal connections are never allowed, each player can adopt a
simple strategy that assures the other player cannot win: suppose the
game is played on an NxN square grid. (This might be an unwarranted
assumption- maybe the overall shape of the grid is a diamond, as in
Bridgit?) Consider two adjacent rows that run from one of your borders
to the other. Now partition this region into N "dominoes," each
pointing towards your opponent's borders. In order for your opponent
to win, he/she must occupy both parts of one of those dominoes. But if
you always respond to a move in one half of a domino by playing in the
other half, your opponent will be blocked. The game should be a draw.
So, to answer your question, Hex on a square lattice, if this is what
you're talking about, is not complex enough to be p-space complete.
