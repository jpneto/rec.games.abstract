The distinction between Strategy and Tactics is originally a
military one; Strategy is saying "Let's bomb their supply lines from the
air." whereas Tactics is saying "Use 12 F-15e's to bomb the supply line
at coordinates X-Y with mavericks."
Strategy is the plan formulated by generals whereas Tactics is
the more local planning and execution put down by Liutenants and
Seargents.
For example in chess; Strategy is to play aggressively, in your
opponents half, all pieces being defended; Tactics is to put THAT rook
where it's defended by the pawn and the bishop and has vantage towards
your opponnents king and rook.
Having said that, the line where Strategy becomes Tactics is
very hard to place, and since one does not succeed well without the
other then, semantics aside, does it really matter at all? Insofar as
games refer to Strategy and Tactics, they do not do so in the rules and
so, the distinction is moot in playing the game. As for commentary on
games, since every case would be explained anyway, it isn't so bad if
we occasionaly err in our ways.
