Yep, right. We play this on 9x9 every so often after tournament sessions.

It's remarkably even between black and white. The usual move is to play
two stones touching one another, somewhere; but by no means always.

Seki exists, most simply where two eyeless groups have three common liberties.

Ko is interesting. Using the (most natural) super-ko rule, i.e. that you
may not leave (after both moves) a position you've left before, ordinary
SINGLE kos from the regular game are no longer kos; but there ARE still
ko situations. I'll leave it as an exercise to construct one.

There are often subtleties where there is a distinct choice between playing
a sente full move, or two semi-sente single moves; especially in relation
to ko threats!

For a bit more info about all this, see my accompanying post "Go variants".
