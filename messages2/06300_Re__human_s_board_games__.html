No. You might be thinking of Connections, a.k.a. Connexxions a.k.a. Bridg-it
a.k.a. Gale, which is superficially similar to Twixt. A winning strategy for
that game is described here:

http://www.flash.net/~markthom/html/bridg-it.html

But Twixt admits of no such resolution. Certainly, without the swap rule,
the player who moves first in Twixt would have a strong winning advantage,
but swap is part and parcel of Alex Randolph's creation. And even without
allowing swap (which Randolph calls the "pie rule") the win for the first
player is not nearly as easy as some might think. For example, an
experienced player could give a newbie a four row plus first move handicap,
and would probably still win.

Yes Jan, a draw is certainly more likely in Twixt with swap than without,
but it is far from clear whether "perfect play" would result in a draw
game. On a practical level, draws are rare even among experienced
players, but there really aren't enough of us to have gone very far in
opening theory. Nor are we likely to get very far as long as the only
servers for the game are email or turn-based.

But returning to the original question of this post, I think a strong Twixt
program could be written eventually, which most humans would lose against.
That's not really saying very much, though, even if you restrict the scope
of that statement to those humans who know how to play the game.
