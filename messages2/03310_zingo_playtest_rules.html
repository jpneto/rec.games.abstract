The Single-Word, 5-letter word, secret word games
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

In these games, there is a secret word that you
must discover by the application of test words
- one at a time per secret word - that themselves
would be legal secret words. Each time you apply
a test word, you are told the count of letters
that appear in both the secret word and the test
word. When the result is a '5', you have found
the secret word - even though the test word is
just an anagram of the secret word; stare, stear,
rates, etc are equivalent. Your score on a secret
word is the number of test words you used to get
a '5'. Secret: horse; test: songs; result: 2.

There will be a multiple of eight secret words
in every set provided. Your score on each set
will be the sum of the individual secret word
scores.

As of the date of this revision, sets will be
of eight words each, and each set score will
be the sum of the eight individual scores.

These set scores will position you on a 'ladder'
that will be the eventual basis for ratings like
those of the United States Chess Federation, and
will provide the basis eventually for ladder
challenge play, a standard form of club competi-
tion in which you may challenge the players very
close to your position on the ladder, and take
their place if you beat them.


ZINGO: 5-letter words; zero duplicate letters
allowed. The word 'zingo' contains no
duplicate letters. Formal sets of secret
words will have a title that begins with
WG5S.ZD. WG=word game; 5S=5-letter words,
a single word per 'game'; ZD=zero dupli-
cates.

GINGO: 5-letter words; one duplicate letter
ALLOWED but NOT required; all the words
that are legal in zingo are also legal
in gingo, but in gingo even more are
legal. The word 'gingo' contains one
letter duplicated, the 'G'. Formal sets
of secret words will hve a title that
begins with WG5S.SD. SD=single duplicate
allowed.

GONGO: 5-letter words; two letters duplicated
are allowable, but not required; all
words that are legal in gingo are legal
in gongo. The word 'gongo' contains two
duplicated letters, the 'G's and the 'O's.
Formal sets of secret words will have a
title that begins with WG5S.DD. DD=double
duplicate allowed. Further, however, words
with tripled letters are also allowed; in
fact, every word in the official word list
is legal in GONGO.

The informal names of the games, then, SHOW you
what is allowed as a legal word. The formal names
TELL you what is legal.



The Official Word List
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Only words in the provided word list are allowable
secret or test words. The secret words are randomly
chosen by the brain club computer, you chose the
test words!

The list of 4204 words includes some words not likely
to be used in normal conversation or writing, but
everyone is on the same foot.

Perhaps one is mis-spelled? We don't know that. But
everyone is on the same foot.

Our programs do not count illegal words against your
score.

And do not count a test word twice if you accidently
try to use it more than once on the same secret word.

This means that you may mail the same test words to
us more than once to make sure we get them and the
only problem will be that the brain club program has
to process it twice, and mail the results to you twice.

The word list comes in two forms. In one, the words
are in alphabetical order. In the other, a file that
is twice as large, the words are in order of the five
letters of the word after those letters have been
alphabetized themselves: awash => aahsw; baker =>
abekr; etc.


Two approaches to playing WG5S games:

In both, you start with high-frequency letters.

Eleaticus - who is not eligible for any prizes
if brain club WG5S games get that far - only
played zingo in the past and always started
with words like (train/louse) and (snail/route).

Any of those four words would be the first test
word, and the second would usually be the other
word in the pair; in which case the third test
word would likely be a truly possible combination
of the letters in the first two tests.

For example:

train 2
louse 2
baler ?

'baler' has two letters (a, r) from train and
two (l, e) from louse.

When testing the computer program in various
ways, Eleaticus decided to try a different
method even on zingo: start with a very high
frequency word, and on the second word start
with actual possibilities. The second word in
the train/louse, snail/route technique is not
a possible secret word after anything but a
zero result for the first test word.

Eleaticus' two zingo results during testing
were 66 and 71 with this second method.

Using email to play
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
When you get an email response from brain
club showing you the results of your latest
(and previous) tests, there is very little
you have to do to make the next tries. You
do not have to edit-out anything you don't
want to mess with. That includes both special
characters your reader inserts to tell you
what it is quoting from brain club's email,
and any and all lines you don't really need
in your response.

Just

(1) replace a certain * with the 'private
name' (a password, in effect) that you
have already given us. This is on a
line that includes: "[]NAME: * ".

(2) for each of the secret words in the set
on which you wish to make a new test,
replace an * with the new test word on
the line that (for game 7) includes:
"[]GAME 7: * ".

(3) if something did go haywire with the
computer program, and a []GAME line
does not appear in the brain club email
to you, just add it yourself if you want
to make a new test on that word.

(4) Rule (B) may require a little editing.


Rules:

(A) Only one set of secret words in each
email you send us.

(B) The Subject: line will always include
the 'public name' you have given us,
with or without the 'Re:' mailers insert
in fromt of the subject as it came to you
from brain club. If your reader/mailer
inserts something else, get rid of it,
but tell us what it is so we can remove
that as a problem for both of us.

(C) The []NAME: line must be present; it
works as a password.

(D) The []BEGIN: line must be present; it
tells the computer what set of secret
words and rules to use.

(E) The []END: line must be present. Actually,
the program does not always require it, but
there may well be places in the computer
programming that will mess up if it isn't
there.

(F) If you want the NEXT response to you to
go to a different email address, change
the * on the []ADDRESS: * line to the
new address. We do not mail responses to
the incoming From: address; our game mail
will always go to the address on record.
If you want an immediate change of address,
that means you first send us the address
change without a new test word, then you
send us new test words. But remember, you
can always try the same test words twice
without penalty.

At present, every game email you send us will
result in brain club showing you all the pre-
vious tests on every word not yet found.
