Triggered by the previous post regarding the game hnefatafl, I present
this document which is based on the rules in danish that accompanies
the commercial game nefatavl produced by the magazine "Skalk" in Den-
mark. If you want to buy it, try to contact Skalk, Jelshoejvaenget 29,
DK 8270 Hoejbjerg, Denmark. They also have a phone +45 86 27 37 11 and
a fax +45 86 27 30 46. No e-mail, I'm afraid.
I got the game for my birthday, and I think it is nice. The men are made
of wood in a simple design, while the board is made of cardboard and
decorated with ornaments from a danish wiking's axe. As I got it as a
present, I don't know the price, but it's probably around 200 dkr, which
is close to 40 dollars.
As one can see, this game differs a bit from the original one presented,
and some of the ideas of Torben Mogensen are similar to some of the ones
in this version. Personally, I prefer this version because of the simpler
initial setup, but both games are quite enjoyable. At least, this version
is well-balanced when you progress to be a little better than novice.
Maybe someone could do a computer version or a PBE-version?

Nefatavl
--------

History
-------

The game nefatavl (or hnefatafl) is an ancient game from Northern Europe
which was popular a few thousand years ago. We know the game from fin-
dings and deliveries, but only one of these sources give a reasonable
idea of the rules of the game: The swedish scientist Carl von Linn
wrote about it after a visit to the Lapps in 1732. The game he saw there
was without a doubt nefatavl, but the rules have probably changed through
history. Among those who have studied nefatavl, we should mention the
english archeologist David Brown.
The setting of the game is a raid against a castle, where the king
recides. If the attackers capture the king, they win, while the defenders
win, if the king manages to escape. The game differs from most other
boardgames because the two players have different numbers of men,
different starting positions, and different goals for winning.
Beginners will prefer to play the defenders role, but this inbalance
will quickly disappear with practice.
The rules presented here mainly follow those dictated by Carl von Linn.
As a real board has never been found, the board is a reconstruction.

Rules
-----

The board has 11x11 squares. There are 24 white and 13 black men. One of
the black men is the king, which is larger than the rest. They are initially
placed like this:

abcdefghijk
+-----------+
1|...ooooo...|
2|.....o.....|
3|...........|
4|o....x....o|
5|o...xxx...o|
6|oo.xx+xx.oo|
7|o...xxx...o|
8|o....x....o|
9|...........|
10|.....o.....|
11|...ooooo...|
+-----------+


Here, the "o"s illustrate the white men, while the "x"s illustrate the
black men. The "+" is the black king and the dots are empty squares.
The white men are the attackers and start the game.

All men (including the king) move like rooks in chess: north, south, east
or west, never diagonally. White and black takes turns, and all men can
move any number of squares as long as they don't leap over any other men,
neither black nor white.

Five squares have special rules: The four corners and the center square
can only be accessed by the king. All men (including the king) can pass
the center square if it's empty, but only the king can occupy it.

The men capture the opponent men (except the king) by sandwiching them:
A man is captured if it is caught between two of the opponent men.

..... ..... ......
.ox.. .oxo. .o.o..
..... -> ..... -> ......
...o. ..... ......
..... ..... ......

White moves... ...and captures the black man.


A man can freely place itself between two opponents without loosing his life:

..... .....
.o.o. .oxo.
..... -> .....
..x.. .....
..... .....

Black moves, but is not captured.


You can thus capture up to three men in a single move:

..... ..... .....
...o. ..... .....
..... ..... .....
.ox.. -> .oxo. -> .o.o.
...x. ...x. .....
...o. ...o. ...o.
..... ..... .....

White moves... ...and captures the two black men.


....... ....... .......
...x... ...x... ...x...
...o... ...o... .......
.xo..x. -> .xox... -> .x.x...
...o... ...o... .......
...x... ...x... ...x...
....... ....... .......

Black moves... ...and captures all three white men.


You can only capture one man in a row:

...... ......
.xoo.. .xoox.
...... -> ......
....x. ......
...... ......

Black moves, but white is not captured.


The corners and the center square (if it is empty) act as men when
capturing:

+------ +------ +------
|.x... |.xo.. |..o..
|..... -> |..... -> |.....
|..o. |.... |....
|.... |.... |....

White moves, and captures the black man, which is next to
the corner.


....... ....... .......
....... ....... .......
....... ....... .......
..oc... -> .xoc... -> .x.c...
....... ....... .......
.x..... ....... .......
....... ....... .......

Black moves, and captures the white man, which is next to
the empty center square marked by "c".


The king captures men like all other men, but the king itself is
harder to capture. White wins if it captures the king, which can be
done normally, by sandwiching it with two real men or one man and
a corner, but the center square can not be used for capturing as above.
Rather the king has to be surrounded if it is on or adjacent to the
center square. I.e. if white move and create a situation as these,
white wins:

....... .......
....... .......
..o.... ...o...
.o+c... ..o+o..
..o.... ...o...
....... .......
....... .......

King next to King on
center square center square

Black wins if the king manages to escape to any of the four corners.
If one of the players is unable to move, the opposite side wins.
