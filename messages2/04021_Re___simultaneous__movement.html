You're right- in chess, there are situations where such an idea would be
awkward to implement. For example, Alice (white) is trying to castle
(kingside), while simultaneously Bob (black) is trying check Alice.
Does Bob's move "retroactively" prevent Alice from castling? Also, ko
situations in go might be problematic.

But nonetheless what interested me is the way that in some tournament
games, when the players agree to take a break, often Alice, the player
who is *about* to move writes down her move and keeps it secret from
Bob, only revealing it when the break is over. Alice has "commited" to
her move, and cannot think about it during the break. While Bob also
cannot think about *his* next move until after the break, because he
doesn't know Alice's move.

As far as game play over the Internet, cryptography has solved such
problems as how to "really" write down your move and commit to it, while
keeping it secret from your opponent.
