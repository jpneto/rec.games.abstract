Arithmetic is of little significance in most branches of Mathematics.
Pull out a text on Calculus, Differential Equations or Partial
Differential Equations and you will find that apart from elementary
operations such as addition and subtraction with whole numbers and
a bit of canceling fractions there is no Arithmetic. Answers are
often left only partially evaluated e.g. sqr root of 2 over pi.
There is however, a great deal of Algebra and Trig. The most
common reason for students to fail Calculus I is weak Trig
and Algebra skills. It is probably true that nearly all mathematicians
have very strong Algebra and Trig skills.

Even in a computational work such as a Numerical Analysis book
(one of the few advanced math topics where decimals are
used), there is very little arithmetic to be done by hand. The
idea being to develop computation schemes where a machine does
all the arithmetic. As a matter of curiosity, I looked thru
my copy of Ralston "First Course In Numerical Analysis" which
was published before computers were freely avaialble. In those
days computation schemes were evaluated by hand and required
a *great deal* of arithmetic. In this book one needs no arithmetic
to read the text and of the problems at the end of each chapter
fewer than one in ten requires any arithmetic computation at all.

There are whole fields in mathematics that deal with objects that
are not numbers e.g. Logic, Topology and Abstract Algebra. In such
work arithmetic is pretty much irrelevant.

One activity that is found in all branches of mathematics is the
proving of theorems. If you don't prove theorems but just use
mathematical ideas then you are not a mathematicians but an
*engineer*. Mathematicians consider it an insult to be called
"an engineer", engineers on the other hand are offended
to called "a mathematician". :)
