Well, I suppose it is not abstract because the game has the concrete
scenario of an adventurer who travels around a dungeon solving puzzles.
But if you fiddle with your monitor just right or take off your glasses,
you will have a bunch of blurry points of color which can then be
manipulated at a purely abstract level.

Okay, I'll be serious. This game I wrote is for Windows and you can
download it for free from http://www.webfootgames.com/catalog/drod.htm .

You control an adventurer who must explore and conquer 350 rooms. The
rooms are like giant chessboards and each one is a unique puzzle. There
are monsters that you must battle. You occupy one square and your sword
occupies an adjacent square. Abstractly speaking, you "capture"
monsters by moving your sword piece into the square they occupy, but you
must not allow monsters to move into your square. Play is turn-based,
but since the monsters move immediately after you do, there are no
delays except when you stop to think. It feels a little like playing
speed chess, but you have as much time as you want.

There are eleven different monsters, who all have different abilities or
behavior, and also lots of other game elements--doors that open when
criteria are met, squares that can be stepped on only once, one-way
arrows, and much more.

Try it out! The free shareware version has the first 3 levels which are
pretty easy. Also, check out rec.games.computer.puzzle--this is where
everybody has been asking for help on different rooms.
