For those not in the know, Progressive Chess is a very simple concept:-

The rules are as in ordinary chess, except that white starts with one
move,
then black plays 2 moves, then white 3, black 4, and so on. Check may
not
be given except as the last move of a sequence, and must be relieved by
the first move of the next sequence. So checkmate is as usual.

Simple! But hot! (There are more details of course, but the full rules

will be made available via the mailing list and
web site.)

The 1996 championship, won by Fred Galvin, had *60* participants,
making it one of the largest email tournaments ever held for a chess
variant!

The organizer for the 1998 championship is Doug Hyatt
(hyatt@cs.utk.edu). Send all registrations and any questions you may
have to hyatt@cs.utk.edu.

As a taster, here is maybe the shortest game ever seriously played, from

the 1st internet championship, by players who shall remain nameless.

1. e4 (still quite a common opening - & threatens fools
mate!)
2. f6 Nc6 (prevents that, & guards against a 3-move Q capture)
3. Qg4 Qxg7 Qxh8 (what an idiot! - at least leave your opponent in
check!)
4. d5 Bg4 Nd4 Nxc2 (AAAAAARRRRGGGHHH!!!!)
