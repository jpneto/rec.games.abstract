Okay, for those wondering exactly what this is about, I checked it out about
a week ago and am rapidly getting very, very hooked!
It can be a bit hard at first to figure out what exactly you're trying to
solve, and how to get into the whole thing, but once you get underway, plot
momentum really starts rolling and the atmosphere is marvellous. By all
means visit the Jawbreakers group for help but dont just read the solutions
before trying puzzles for yourself.
This is admittedly very different to the IF that this group is dedicated to,
and initially i was a bit "oh, another off-topic post of someone advertising
their own little site" but give it a chance. If you really enjoy cracking a
good IF puzzle, you will have fun with this. But this is just my opinion,
and as always with someone's opinion, *don't* just accept it as truth but
see for yourself.

> New Online Mystery Game
>
> A new, interactive online alternate reality game codenamed
> Lockjaw went live on the internet on February 7, 2002.
>
> Authored by a team of mystery developers who were involved
> in the online promotional mystery game for the film A.I.,
> Lockjaw was originally revealed in a Wired article
> (http://www.wired.com/news/games/0,2101,46672,00.html) in
> September of 2001. The game centers around a fictional
> biotech company named Ganmed, and a group of urban
> explorers calling themselves the DC Metrocrawlers. Players
> must explore websites and sometimes interact with in-game
> characters to discover clues that lead them further into
> the story. However, this is NOT your typical linear
> adventure game. The storyline may make twists and turns
> depending upon discoveries and decisions made by the
> players.
>
> Members of Cloudmakers, the online community that grew
> around the addictive A.I. game, have branched off to form
> Jawbreakers, a community of online detectives to discuss,
> discover and solve Lockjaw. Anybody can join this group at
> http://groups.yahoo.com/group/jawbreak/ .
>
> Lockjaw follows in the footsteps of it's A.I. predecessor
> by communicating with its players through a variety of
> media, including websites, emails, phone calls, and perhaps
> even clues left in the real world. While some puzzles can
> be solved by individual players, others need the collective
> knowledge and resources of the Jawbreakers, made up of
> members from around the world.
>
> This game is free, and anyone can participate. To check
> out what's going on, go to the Jawbreakers Quickstart Guide at
> http://www.vpmusic.com/jawbreakers/quickstart.htm, or to the
> Jawbreakers Group at
> http://groups.yahoo.com/group/jawbreak/ .
