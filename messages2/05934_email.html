from a couple different sources. The game Tablut was recorded in the diary
of the Swedish botanist Linnaeus in 1732. He found the game being played by
Alpine Lapplanders and recorded the set up and a description of the rules.
An even earlier description of Hnefatafl-type game survives in an English
manuscript from the reign of King Athelstan (A.D. 925-40). It includes a
diagram of a board and the starting positions on a 19 x 19 grid and at
least partial rules for the game. There's a photo of the MS page in R.C.
Bell's "Board & Table Games from Many Civilizations." But I've always
assumed these may have been folk games with rules that may have varied
from one village to the next. I like to say that games are like languages
-they have dialects that change as they move from place to place.
