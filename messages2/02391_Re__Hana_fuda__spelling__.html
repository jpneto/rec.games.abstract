Hana Fuda ( which is as good a transliteration of the Japanese as
any ) is a Japanese card game played with a special set of small
( about 1"3/4 by 2"1/2 ) cards mounted on a rather stiff card
stock. The pictures on the cards are all flowers, and animals and
the "suits" are based on the 12 months of the year. The only
specific ones I remember are the Plum Blossom set which corres-
ponds to February, the month it blossoms, and the Pine bough set,
which corresponds to January, when it is the only green thing
around. All the sets are like this, though. The cards are used for
many different games, all of them are closely related to gambl-
ing, and seem to be related to the blackjack/baccarat family of
trying to get enough but not too much. You can buy these cards in
in almost any convenience store in Japan for about $5 US.

There is a popular coin-op computer version of the game in Japan
where you play against a computer dealer usually in order to get
a "virtual lady" to strip down as you win. The cards are lovely
anyway, and yet the rules to this particular version baffle me,
and I never found anyone who could explain it to me, but this
version did not play like blackjack, but was based on some really
complex set of set matching rules.

As to Nintendo's involvement, they make all the cards in Japan.
Yes, all. They make these playing cards that are bridge-sized, and
100% plastic, never wears out, even use them as coasters. Most of
the large corporation over there have there fingers in many pies.
For example, Mitsubishi is also the pencil meister. Strange place.
But always interesting.
